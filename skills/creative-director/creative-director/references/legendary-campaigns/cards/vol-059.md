# Pinterest Yard Sale (Krylon · 2013)

## Insight
People pin DIY inspiration on Pinterest constantly but rarely act on it. Krylon spray paint is the tool that closes the gap between pinned aspiration and real transformation.

## Mechanic
Krylon created the "first ever Pinterest yard sale" — buying cheap yard sale items, spray painting them in Krylon colours, pinning the before/after transformations, and selling the upgraded items. Each pin linked directly to the product used.

## Why it worked
The mechanic was native to Pinterest's format and psychology. It demonstrated product efficacy through real transformation, not claims. The social loop (pin → buy → transform → re-pin) was self-sustaining and generated genuine product proof.

## Steal
Build your campaign inside a platform's native behaviour loop rather than advertising on top of it. When the campaign format is indistinguishable from organic platform use, it earns rather than interrupts attention.

# Save Our Species (Lacoste · 2018)

## Insight
Lacoste's crocodile logo — one of fashion's most recognisable icons — is a species itself. Ten endangered species had smaller surviving populations than Lacoste had produced polo shirts that season.

## Mechanic
For a limited edition collection, Lacoste replaced its iconic crocodile logo with ten endangered species — each species' representation proportional to its surviving wild population. The rarest species had the fewest polo shirts made. Sold through IUCN partnership. Sold out immediately.

## Why it worked
Design as idea (P17): the logo replacement was the entire campaign. No film, no headline could do what the visual substitution accomplished in a single glance. Brand as activist (P04): the sacrifice was real — Lacoste surrendered its most valuable asset (the logo) to make the point credible.

## Steal
When a brand's most iconic visual element can become a medium for a message, the act of substituting it is more powerful than any communication built around it. The willingness to give up the logo for a purpose signals genuine commitment over stated values.

# The Big Leap (Lacoste · 2021)

## Insight
Life's pivotal moments feel like stepping off a cliff — the moment between commitment and consequence, when everything could go either way.

## Mechanic
A film built around the surreal, extended visual of characters leaping — the absurd logic (P11) of an impossible, elongated fall becomes a metaphor for life's risks. Long form allows the absurdist premise to breathe and accumulate meaning rather than land as a punchline.

## Why it worked
Lacoste's consistent long-form investment signals brand confidence. The absurdist visual language (P11) differentiates from the earnest competitor films in fashion, while the emotional undertow of risk-and-reward grounds the surrealism in something universally felt.

## Steal
Absurdism works in luxury when it's internally consistent — one surreal premise followed to its logical visual conclusion without explanation. The moment a fashion brand explains the metaphor, the spell breaks.

# Timeless (Lacoste · 2017)

## Insight
Some products outlast the moments they were born in — they become witnesses to an entire life. The crocodile polo isn't just clothing; it's a recurring character in someone's personal history.

## Mechanic
A long-form film that follows a couple's relationship across decades, with the Lacoste polo shirt as the recurring visual anchor through changing decades, fashions, and life events. The garment's constancy is the implicit product claim.

## Why it worked
The serialization mechanic (P10) applied within a single film: repetition of the same object across time creates cumulative emotional weight. Long-form drama (P16) allows the relationship narrative to build to the point where the polo is genuinely moving rather than merely present.

## Steal
A single object repeated across a long-form timeline does more brand-building work than any product demonstration. The object becomes a character; the brand becomes part of memory rather than marketing.

# Unexpected Encounters (Lacoste · 2023)

## Insight
The most memorable human connections are the ones we don't plan — brief meetings that leave a permanent emotional trace, impossible to explain but impossible to forget.

## Mechanic
A cinematic film following two strangers through an extended, unplanned encounter — structured around craft of performance and cinematography rather than product logic. Extended format, minimal Lacoste branding, maximum emotional investment in the narrative.

## Why it worked
Craft as message (P08): Lacoste consistently invests in film craft as a signal of brand quality without making product-centric claims. The long-form structure (P16) earns emotional complexity unavailable in shorter formats. The brand's "croc" is present as a quiet signature rather than a shouted logo.

## Steal
When a brand's equity is built on style and elegance, the ad itself must demonstrate those qualities through production choices — director, soundtrack, pace, colour grading — not through assertions in a voiceover.
