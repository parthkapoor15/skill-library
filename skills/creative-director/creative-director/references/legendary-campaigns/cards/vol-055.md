# The Boy & the Piano (John Lewis & Partners · 2018)

## Insight
A gift at the right moment can unlock the trajectory of an entire life — John Lewis as the place where those formative gifts are found.

## Mechanic
A film tracing Elton John's life in reverse from his global fame back to a young boy receiving a piano as a Christmas gift from his grandmother — that single present the origin point of everything. Elton John narrates and performs.

## Why it worked
Reverse chronology from peak achievement to childhood gift makes the product's emotional argument concrete: this is what a gift can do. Celebrity casting elevated to genuine personal biography rather than endorsement. The grandmother becomes the hero.

## Steal
Reverse-trace a famous outcome to the gift or moment that triggered it. The argument becomes: ordinary acts of giving have extraordinary long-term consequences. Celebrity biography as product proof.

# The Long Wait (John Lewis · 2011)

## Insight
Christmas excitement is typically assumed to be about receiving gifts — but what if a child's impatience was actually about the anticipation of giving?

## Mechanic
A film of a young boy barely sleeping, watching the clock, unable to contain himself through Christmas — the audience assumes he is desperate to receive presents. The reveal: he can't wait to give his parents the gift he's chosen for them.

## Why it worked
The inversion of the assumed motivation (selfish desire → generous love) lands as an emotional twist that reframes the entire preceding film. The reveal is not a plot trick — it is an argument about what Christmas actually means, delivered through a child's behaviour.

## Steal
Structure a narrative around an assumed motivation, then invert it in the final beats. The audience's complicity in the wrong interpretation makes the correction more emotionally resonant than if the truth had been stated from the start.

# Tiny Dancer (John Lewis · 2023)

## Insight
A child's passionate, untrained expression of joy — especially when it causes minor chaos — is something parents love and fear in equal measure; Christmas gifts that enable that expression are the truest gifts.

## Mechanic
A film following a young girl's uninhibited passion for dancing, which causes havoc throughout the family home. Christmas morning she receives ballet shoes — the first formal acknowledgment and nurturing of her gift.

## Why it worked
The film celebrates uncontrollable childhood passion, which parents simultaneously treasure and manage. The gift resolves the tension: instead of containing the passion, it channels it. The Elton John track "Tiny Dancer" does significant emotional lifting.

## Steal
Find the behaviour parents both love and find exhausting in their children — the trait that is simultaneously a gift and a disruption. Frame the product as the thing that honours rather than suppresses it.

# Bear Fight (John West Salmon · 2000)

## Insight
"It's the fish John West reject that makes John West the best" is a product quality claim so familiar it's meaningless. Making the lengths John West goes to — literally fighting a bear for the best salmon — visible and absurd makes the claim unforgettable.

## Mechanic
A film opening with a plausible nature documentary of a bear catching salmon — then cutting to a fisherman who fights the bear hand-to-hand (including low groin kicks and hair pulling) to get the best fish. The logo: "John West. It's the salmon John West rejects that makes John West the best."

## Why it worked
The nature documentary opening was a deliberate tonal misdirection — the familiar register made the absurd break land harder. The product claim was unchanged; only the dramatisation was new. The fight choreography was committed enough to be genuinely funny and technically impressive.

## Steal
The oldest product claims can be refreshed not by changing the claim but by radicalising the dramatisation. If the claim is "we only take the best," show the most extreme version of that commitment. The audience will remember the bear fight long after they've forgotten the tagline.

# The Gentleman's Wager (Johnnie Walker · 2014)

## Insight
Progress and achievement are most meaningful when they arise from a personal challenge freely taken — a bet between equals. The Johnnie Walker man doesn't succeed for reward; he succeeds to prove something to himself.

## Mechanic
A short film starring Jude Law as a man who wagers a yacht against a private island with a stranger — the wager is to sail across the ocean. The film is cinematic in scale and craft, told as a classic adventure narrative. The whisky appears as context, not product. A sequel followed.

## Why it worked
The "Keep Walking" brand platform (progress without destination) was expressed through action rather than declaration. A bet freely made and honoured regardless of outcome embodied the brand's promise in the oldest male narrative form: the challenge.

## Steal
When a brand's equity is in a philosophy (progress, courage, honour) rather than a product attribute, find the classic narrative form that embodies that philosophy and let the story do the argument. The Odyssey is more convincing than a claim.
