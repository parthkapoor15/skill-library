# What Are Girls Made Of? (Nike Russia · 2017)

## Insight
In Russia, a traditional nursery rhyme assigns girls sugar and spice — passive, sweet qualities. Female athletes in Russia negotiate this cultural framing every day. Nike's Russian campaign had a specific cultural text to invert.

## Mechanic
A film rewriting the traditional Russian "What Are Girls Made Of?" nursery rhyme — replacing the sugar-and-spice answer with the actual stuff of female athletes: sweat, determination, blood, sacrifice, muscle. Specifically Russian cultural reference, universally legible insight.

## Why it worked
P13 (truth-telling) with a specific cultural target: not "girls can be athletes" in the generic global sense, but "the Russian story about girls is wrong." The nursery rhyme gives the campaign a specific text to argue with — which is always more powerful than arguing with a general attitude. The inversion is precise: same structure, opposite content.

## Steal
Find the specific cultural text — a nursery rhyme, a proverb, a saying — that encodes the attitude you're fighting. Invert the text exactly rather than making a general counter-argument. The precision of the target makes the response more powerful.

# Wings (Nike · 1987)

## Insight

Michael Jordan was not simply a basketball player — he was a physical event. The question in 1987 was not how to sell a shoe but how to represent a man whose athletic gifts seemed to exceed the language of sports marketing.

## Mechanic

A poster/OOH execution: Michael Jordan in mid-air, captured in a moment that appears to defy gravity, with the word "Wings" as the only text alongside the Swoosh. The image was taken by photographer Annie Leibovitz. No product visible. No claims. The poster became one of the most iconic sports images of the decade and a collector's item.

## Why it worked

P17 (design as idea): the image is the argument — Jordan in flight, framed as mythological rather than athletic. The word "Wings" transforms the image from sports photography into something closer to religious iconography. P16 (long-form drama): a still image can have narrative — this one implies an entire story of ascent and possibility in a single frame. Nike's restraint (no product, no copy) amplified the mythic quality.

## Steal

When your brand is associated with genuine human excellence, the most powerful communication is the one that comes closest to pure image — removing all commercial framing and allowing the person to exist as a cultural figure rather than a brand vehicle.

# Write the Future (Nike · 2010)

## Mechanic
A three-minute Super Bowl film for the 2010 World Cup showing the branching consequences of a single moment in football — what happens to a player, a country, a civilization if one shot goes in or misses. Featuring Wayne Rooney, Didier Drogba, Cristiano Ronaldo, Fabio Cannavaro. Each decision branches into an alternate history of glory or disgrace.

## Insight
The World Cup is not just a sporting event — it is a moment that rewrites nations' stories of themselves. One kick can determine what a country believes about itself for years. Nike's athletes are not playing a game; they are writing history.

## Why it worked
The branching timeline structure makes the stakes of football explicitly civilizational, not sporting. The film earns its three-minute length through escalation — each branch going further into absurdist consequence. The craft (cameos, period detail, parallel cutting) demonstrates Nike's investment in the sport at the level of the sport itself.

## Steal
For global sporting events with genuine cultural stakes, don't show the game — show what the game decides about the world. Escalating consequence beyond the field of play is the only way to match the actual emotional weight of the event for committed fans.

# Prescribed to Death (NSC · 2018)

## Insight
22,000 Americans die annually from prescription opioid overdoses. That number is cited in every article about the crisis and remembered by no one — statistics are the format in which horror becomes deniable.

## Mechanic
A wall installation in Chicago's Millennium Park: 22,000 prescription pills, each engraved with the face of a real person who died from opioid overdose. A machine drilled a new pill into the wall every 24 minutes — the rate of new deaths. Visitors could look up individual names. The installation grew in real time as they watched.

## Why it worked
Truth-telling (P13): each pill was a specific face — the abstraction of "22,000" became 22,000 individual legible losses. Design as idea (P17): the wall itself was the message — no copy was needed. The real-time drilling machine made time itself a component of the installation, converting a passive statistic into active accumulation.

## Steal
Give abstract statistics a physical equivalent that grows or changes in real time. Passive statistics are processed and forgotten; active physical objects accumulating in front of an audience create witnesses rather than readers.

# InstaNovels (New York Public Library · 2018)

## Insight
Classic literature is perceived as requiring effort — time, commitment, a dedicated reading environment. Instagram Stories are designed for distracted, sequential, mobile-first scrolling. The NYPL's insight: the format of Instagram Stories is closer to the experience of reading a serialised novel than any other digital format — it forces linear reading and creates the habit of return.

## Mechanic
The NYPL published full classic novels — Alice in Wonderland, The Yellow Wallpaper, The Metamorphosis — formatted as illustrated Instagram Stories. Each novel was designed with original art by contemporary illustrators, formatted to be read one "page" per Story tap, with the full text available through the NYPL account.

## Why it worked
P17 (design as idea): the format decision IS the idea — the Story format turns passive scrolling into active reading without requiring a conscious choice to "sit down and read a book." P09 (user as co-author): audiences shared their progress and discussed the texts, turning consumption into community. The campaign added 150K+ Instagram followers in the first week and won every major show it entered. Zero media budget.

## Steal
Look at the existing behaviours your audience already has and ask which format most closely replicates the experience you're trying to create. If you can put your content into a format that feels native rather than educational, adoption is effortless.
