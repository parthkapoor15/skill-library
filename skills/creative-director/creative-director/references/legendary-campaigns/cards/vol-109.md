# Unfiltered History Tour (VICE · 2022)

## Insight
The British Museum holds thousands of artefacts removed from colonised countries — a permanent, unchallenged historical injustice displayed as culture. The museum's own audio guides present only the coloniser's narrative.

## Mechanic
An unofficial AR audio tour of the British Museum: visitors pointed their phones at specific exhibits and received alternative narrations — voices of historians and descendants from the countries of origin (India, Egypt, Nigeria, etc.) telling the real history of each object's acquisition. Distributed as an Instagram filter, bypassing the institution entirely.

## Why it worked
Technology as canvas (P18): AR made an illegal counter-narrative possible inside the museum itself without physical confrontation. Truth-telling (P13): the alternative histories were rigorously documented, not polemic. The institution's own space became the medium for its critique.

## Steal
When an institution controls the official narrative, use technology to distribute an unauthorised counter-narrative through the same physical space. The institution's legitimacy becomes the backdrop for the critique rather than a barrier to it.

# Pink Moon (Volkswagen · 1999)

## Insight
Young people in a Volkswagen Golf are driving at night, and one by one they choose not to attend the party they were heading to — opting instead to simply drive, watch the pink moon rise, and be together in that moment. The insight: the car is not transportation to a destination; sometimes the journey is the destination, and the people you're with are the point.

## Mechanic
A 60-second film showing a group of twentysomethings in a Golf driving through a dark landscape to Nick Drake's "Pink Moon." They arrive at a party, see the lights and people inside, look at each other — and drive on. No dialogue. No voiceover. No product claims. Just music, the car, and the choice to keep going.

## Why it worked
P16 long-form at its most restrained: 60 seconds was long enough to establish the emotional world, and the silence (no dialogue) made the characters' unspoken communication the story. The VW Golf's positioning as the choice of people who think for themselves — who don't do what they're supposed to do — was communicated entirely through behavior. Nick Drake's "Pink Moon" became iconic partly through this ad. The film earned Cannes Gold and remains one of the most cited examples of automotive advertising that isn't about the car.

## Steal
When the insight is about freedom from obligation, show characters choosing that freedom in a specific, believable moment rather than asserting it as a claim. The choice is more powerful than any statement.

# Sunday Afternoon (Volkswagen · 2003)

## Insight
The "Drivers Wanted" / Golf campaign era established VW as the car for people who drive for the pleasure of it, not merely to arrive. Sunday afternoon is the temporal space of aimless, pleasurable movement — no destination required.

## Mechanic
A long-form film in the mode of VW's "Pink Moon" and "Big Day" — quiet, music-driven, character-focused — using Sunday afternoon as the emotional context for a drive in a VW Golf. P16 long-form with minimal product presence.

## Why it worked
VW's Golf campaign established a consistent aesthetic (quiet, observational, character-revealing) that allowed multiple films to build a cumulative brand world. "Sunday Afternoon" extends that world through the specific emotional texture of aimless weekend driving — a pleasure most people recognize but few advertisers have claimed.

## Steal
A strong brand aesthetic world lets you make multiple films that feel like chapters of the same story. The brand voice — not the product — is the constant.

# The Epic Split (Volvo Trucks · 2013)

## Insight
B2B truck buyers need to trust that precision steering can be relied upon over millions of kilometres. No spec sheet makes this claim viscerally credible. A demonstration that is physically impossible to fake does.

## Mechanic
Jean-Claude Van Damme performs a full lateral split between two reversing Volvo trucks at dawn, filmed in a single continuous take. The film demonstrates the trucks' Dynamic Steering system through an act of genuine physical extremity — not CGI, not editing.

## Why it worked
The demonstration is undeniable: Van Damme's body at full extension between two moving trucks cannot be faked in camera. The cinematic beauty of the execution (dawn, Enya, slow motion) transforms a product claim into a piece of film worth watching independently. Targeted at fleet buyers, it overdelivered to a global consumer audience.

## Steal
Find the product attribute that can be demonstrated in a way that would be physically impossible without the product's actual performance. Make the demonstration beautiful enough that it travels beyond the category audience.
