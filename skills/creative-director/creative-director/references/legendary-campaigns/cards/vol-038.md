# Childlike Imagination (GE · 2014)

## Insight
A child who grows up to work at GE sees the company as her parent did — through a lens of limitless possibility. The gap between "what GE actually does" and "what a child thinks GE does" is smaller than most adults would assume, because GE genuinely does work on things that seem fantastical.

## Mechanic
A long-form film following a young girl who describes her mother's work at GE to other children through the lens of imagination — flying machines, magical engines, medical miracles — and the reveal being that she's not wrong. The industrial reality is as wondrous as the childlike description.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16) gave room for the emotional arc between innocence and industrial truth to land with full weight. The film reframes GE's industrial work as genuinely wonder-worthy — not through a claim but through the perspective of someone for whom wonder is the default.

## Steal
Use a child's literal misunderstanding of a complex product or company as a creative frame. When the child's "wrong" description is actually more accurate than the corporate version, the audience experiences a reframe that works better than any tagline.

# Freak Chain (GE · 2016)

## Insight
GE's innovations in industrial technology — turbines, medical equipment, aviation — are technically extraordinary but invisible to consumer audiences. Making the invisible exciting requires a different register than earnest demonstration.

## Mechanic
A film in the absurdist tradition of GE's broader campaign series at the time — likely featuring surreal product-related chains of cause and effect, or anthropomorphised technology in unexpected situations. Part of GE's work with BBDO exploring ways to make industrial innovation culturally resonant.

## Why it worked
Absurdism (P11) was GE's consistent strategy in this period for making B2B-grade technology accessible to a general audience: instead of explaining what the technology does, make the audience feel the spirit of ingenuity through bizarre, surprising content.

## Steal
When technology is too complex to explain accessibly, use absurdism to communicate the emotional quality of the innovation — curiosity, ingenuity, surprise — rather than its specifications.

# Lightning (GE · 2016)

## Insight
GE's power-generation technology is about harnessing elemental forces — electricity, turbines, energy at scale. Lightning is electricity at its most primal and spectacular. What if you took that metaphor literally?

## Mechanic
A film from GE's BBDO absurdist series, centred on lightning as a visual and conceptual metaphor — likely exploring electricity's raw power through a surreal extended scenario that connects natural electrical phenomena to GE's industrial harnessing of the same force.

## Why it worked
Absurdism (P11) allows GE to connect its industrial technology to something universally spectacular (lightning) without a technical explanation. The visual spectacle is the argument. Long-form (P16) gives the metaphor enough runtime to build from wonder to product connection.

## Steal
Natural phenomena (lightning, fire, water) are pre-existing spectacular visual assets. If your product works with or through those phenomena, using them as the creative premise costs nothing but stakes the product claim on the audience's existing awe.

# Snowball (GE · 2016)

## Insight
Technology's momentum, once started, builds on itself — innovations compounding like a snowball gaining mass as it rolls. GE's ecosystem of interconnected technologies is larger than any single product.

## Mechanic
A film in GE's absurdist campaign series, likely exploring the snowball metaphor — technology or consequence accumulating with surreal, unstoppable momentum. Part of the BBDO creative series that made GE technology culturally accessible through extended absurdist storytelling.

## Why it worked
Absurdism (P11) as metaphor: using a snowball's physical properties (growth, momentum, unstoppability) to communicate an abstract idea about technological compounding. Long-form format (P16) allows the metaphor to build to a satisfying, surreal conclusion.

## Steal
Physical metaphors (snowball, domino, chain) are inherently absurdist when taken to their logical visual extreme. Use them to communicate abstract brand concepts — compounding, momentum, inevitability — without asserting them in voiceover.

# The Message (GE · 2015)

## Insight
GE's core business is systems that work invisibly — power grids, aviation, medical imaging — things that only become visible when they fail. The most compelling proof of GE's value is the story of what its technology enables for actual human beings.

## Mechanic
A long-form narrative film exploring what happens when GE's technology connects previously isolated communities or enables discoveries that change individual lives. Executed in the style of a documentary or dramatic narrative, centred on a specific human story that GE's infrastructure makes possible.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16) allows GE to escape from product demonstration into human consequence — the actual downstream effect of industrial technology on specific people. The brand's invisibility in the narrative is a feature: GE is the platform, not the protagonist.

## Steal
Industrial and infrastructure brands can build emotional equity by making visible the specific human story that happens because the system worked. The brand is the enabler; the human is the hero.
