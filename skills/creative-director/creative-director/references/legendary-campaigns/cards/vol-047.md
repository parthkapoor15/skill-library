# Pub Museums (Heineken · 2022)

## Insight

Historic Irish pubs are disappearing — they carry irreplaceable community memory, but no institution protects them. Heineken, as a pub brand, has authentic standing to act.

## Mechanic

Heineken turned endangered historic Irish pubs into official "museums," registering them as cultural heritage sites with artifacts, plaques, and preservation documentation — making them literally worth protecting.

## Why it worked

Serialization and ritual meets user participation: the campaign created an ongoing ritual of discovery and nomination, giving drinkers agency in preserving something they already loved. The action (preservation) was more valuable than any ad.

## Steal

If your brand is linked to a physical space that is disappearing, make preservation the campaign. The brand becomes the protector, not the promoter.

# Pub Succession (Heineken · 2023)

## Insight

A beloved historic Irish pub (McLoughlin's) risks closure because there's no heir to carry the name. The cultural loss is real — and findable through modern media.

## Mechanic

Heineken launched a global search for anyone named McLoughlin willing to take over the pub, hijacking the cultural moment of pub-closure anxiety and turning it into a participatory casting call with global media pickup.

## Why it worked

Cultural hijack with genuine stakes: the problem was real, the solution was novel, and the name-search mechanic generated earned media in every Irish diaspora market. User participation (nomination) did the distribution work.

## Steal

Find a real cultural tension in your brand's natural environment (a disappearing place, a dying tradition). Build a stunt that solves it rather than just commenting on it.

# Ketchup Fraud (Heinz · 2023)

## Insight
Restaurants routinely refill Heinz bottles with cheaper generic ketchup — a widespread deception that Heinz fans instinctively detect and resent, but which has never been named publicly.

## Mechanic
Heinz ran a campaign inviting consumers to post evidence of "ketchup fraud" — restaurants caught refilling Heinz bottles with other brands — turning customers into brand detectives and social media became the enforcement mechanism.

## Why it worked
The campaign weaponized existing consumer behaviour (noticing the fraud) by giving it a name, a platform, and a sense of righteous mission. The brand's existing fanbase did the work; Heinz supplied the frame.

## Steal
Find the behaviour your most loyal customers are already doing privately and give it a public name, a hashtag, and a platform. Naming the behaviour creates community; community creates content; content creates reach.

# Major (Hennessy V.S. · 2016)

## Insight
Hennessy's heritage in Black culture and its genuine role in celebrations of achievement — from personal milestones to community victories — is an authentic cultural truth the brand could anchor. "We Do What We Must" positions the brand as companion to genuine striving.

## Mechanic
Long-form film featuring real achievers — likely including music, sport, or cultural figures — whose stories of discipline and triumph are intercut with Hennessy as the drink of earned celebration. The serialization element (P10) suggests this is part of an ongoing brand narrative.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16): achievement stories require extended runtime to convey their emotional weight. Serialization and ritual (P10): the recurring format builds a brand library of achievement stories over time, each adding to the cumulative brand truth. The product earns its place as the drink of genuine achievement rather than aspirational fantasy.

## Steal
For prestige spirits, genuine cultural authenticity — not borrowed credibility — is the only defensible positioning. Build a library of real stories over time rather than a single hero film.

# Role Models (Hillary Clinton · 2016)

## Insight
Children absorb what adults say as normal. An opponent whose rhetoric included racial slurs, mockery of the disabled, and misogyny was not just a political problem — he was a parenting problem.

## Mechanic
A film showing children watching TV footage of the opponent's inflammatory statements — their expressions registering the words. The implicit question: what role model are we presenting? No need to argue policy; the argument is moral and parental.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16): the film's power accumulates through children's reactions rather than through argument or counter-attack. Truth telling (P13): the footage is real; nothing is fabricated. The parental framing transcended political affiliation — it was an appeal to a value (what kind of person do we want our children to become?) that crossed party lines.

## Steal
When the opponent's own words are damaging, the most powerful ad is a documentary compilation of those words with the right witness. Children watching and reacting is the most credible audience proxy because they haven't yet learned to rationalise.
