# Come Out of Nowhere (Nike · 2016)

## Insight
The greatest athletic stories — culturally, emotionally, and commercially — are about the athlete no one expected. Coming out of nowhere is not just an athletic fact; it is the most resonant form of aspiration because it requires no pedigree, no funding, no institutional support.

## Mechanic
A Rio Olympics film featuring athletes from countries not traditionally associated with their sports — unfancied nations, unexpected competitors, surprising medalists. The "come out of nowhere" framing celebrates the underdog as the truest expression of athletic ambition.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16): the individual stories of unexpected athletic achievement require time to establish the obstacle before delivering the inversion. Truth telling (P13): the athletes featured are real; their achievements are documented. "Come out of nowhere" is a phrase that belongs to Nike's defiant register without requiring explanation.

## Steal
The underdog story at the Olympics is renewable every four years with entirely new characters. The insight (anyone can come from nowhere) is universal; the stories change with every Games. Build the platform for the insight, not for the execution.

# Da Da Ding (Nike India · 2016)

## Insight
Indian female athletes are nearly invisible in mainstream media and advertising. Cricket dominates; women's sport barely exists in cultural consciousness. Nike India needed to make female athleticism visible — not in a global aspirational way but in a specifically Indian, visceral, unmissable way.

## Mechanic
A high-energy film featuring Indian female athletes across sports — kabaddi, boxing, football, surfing, wrestling, running — cut to an original track "Da Da Ding" by Generationals, produced specifically for the campaign. The music-video format at 90 seconds, made for mobile consumption. Shot with cinematic intensity that Indian sports advertising had never applied to women.

## Why it worked
P16 (long-form drama) as music video — the format borrowed from entertainment, not advertising, which made it feel like a cultural document rather than a campaign. P13 (truth-telling) through sheer visibility: showing Indian women in these sports at this level of production was itself the news. The track became independently popular. The campaign was the most-viewed Nike film in India at the time.

## Steal
In markets where a demographic is invisible in your category, visibility at high production quality is itself the message. The statement "you exist, and you're extraordinary" is made through the decision to show, not through what the film says.

# Dream Crazier (Nike · 2019)

## Insight
Every adjective used to dismiss women in sport — hysterical, dramatic, delusional — maps exactly to the behaviours celebrated in men: passion, intensity, ambition. The word "crazy" is not an insult; it is a cover story for what the culture cannot accept in women.

## Mechanic
A 90-second film narrated by Serena Williams, debuted during the Oscars, featuring historic and contemporary footage of female athletes being told they're "too emotional," "too aggressive," or "too much." The film inverts each dismissal into a badge of honour, concluding: "It's only crazy until you do it. Just Do It."

## Why it worked
"Dream Crazy" established a platform; "Dream Crazier" made it specific to women without repeating the argument — it extended the insight into a new territory with equal sharpness. P13 (truth-telling) named the language of dismissal precisely, not generally. Serena Williams as narrator was not celebrity endorsement but proof — the most dismissed champion in the sport became the voice of the claim.

## Steal
When a platform lands, the second execution must be more specific, not broader. Extension means taking the original insight and finding the sharpest manifestation in a new audience segment — not diluting the insight to include more people.

# Dream Crazy (Nike · 2018)

## Insight
In a culture of manufactured inspiration, the most radical thing a brand can do is put its name and money behind someone the establishment has punished for their beliefs. Colin Kaepernick was not a risk — he was a proof of brand conviction in the only moment that conviction could be tested.

## Mechanic
Nike launched a 90-second film narrated by Kaepernick — whose NFL career was effectively ended for kneeling during the national anthem — featuring athletes including LeBron James, Serena Williams, and Paralympic athletes. The tagline: "Believe in something. Even if it means sacrificing everything." The film was released on the 30th anniversary of "Just Do It." Nike stock dropped 2% in one day; it rose 36% over the following year.

## Why it worked
P04 (brand as activist) executed at maximum cost — Nike did not choose a safe issue but took a side in an active national controversy. P13 (truth-telling) named what the culture was avoiding: that protesting injustice had become a punishable act. The brand's sacrifice (stock price, consumer boycotts) was the proof that the message was genuine. No campaign in this era more precisely demonstrated the Kaepernick-as-brand-value equation.

## Steal
A brand's willingness to lose something is its credibility. If the social position costs nothing, it signals nothing. Find the issue where your brand's existing customers will push back — and ask whether your conviction is strong enough to absorb that pressure. If it is, the act becomes the brand.

# Find Your Greatness — London (Nike · 2012)

## Insight
During the London 2012 Olympics, Nike was not an official sponsor. Adidas was. Nike could not use Olympic imagery. But Nike recognized that greatness is not a property of the Olympics — it belongs to every person who pushes beyond their limit, anywhere.

## Mechanic
A campaign running during the London Olympics showing ordinary athletes — not Olympians — in places named "London" around the world. "London, Jamaica. London, Ohio. London, Nigeria." Each person performing a modest athletic feat with full personal commitment. The tagline: "Greatness is not some rare DNA strand. It's not a precious thing."

## Why it worked
Nike neutralized an Olympic exclusion by making it philosophically irrelevant. If greatness belongs to everyone, the Olympics is just one expression of it — not the definition. The campaign actually derived more cultural power from not being official: it argued for democratic greatness against elite spectacle.

## Steal
When you cannot compete at the expected level (budget, official status, market position), redefine the level. Shift the definition of the contest so that the expected level stops mattering. Limitation as philosophical reframe, not concession.
