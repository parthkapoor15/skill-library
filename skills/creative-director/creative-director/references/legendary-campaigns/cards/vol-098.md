# The Last Dance (State Farm · 2020)

## Insight
The Michael Jordan documentary aired during lockdown — a moment of collective identity crisis for Americans. State Farm, which had appeared in ESPN broadcasts throughout the Jordan era, had an authentic archival presence in that cultural memory.

## Mechanic
A deepfake-enhanced recreation of an ESPN segment from 1998 in which "commentators of the time" discussed the future — which was the audience's present. State Farm was already there then and is still there now: the through-line is brand permanence. The segment was designed to blend seamlessly with the documentary's archival footage.

## Why it worked
Insurance is a category where stability is the core value proposition but the most boring thing to say. By embedding the brand inside a moment of authenticated nostalgia — using the documentary's own grammar — State Farm proved permanence without asserting it. The deepfake technique was invisible enough to create confusion and visible enough to create conversation.

## Steal
Find a moment in the cultural or category archive where your brand was actually present. Re-stage it so that nostalgia becomes a structural argument for reliability rather than a sentimental ad. The archive is proof; staged sincerity is not.

# Fearless Girl (State Street Global Advisors · 2017)

## Insight
Wall Street's Charging Bull — a symbol of financial aggression and masculine dominance — had stood unchallenged for 27 years. The absence of a counter to that symbol was itself a statement about corporate power. State Street needed to signal its commitment to gender diversity on corporate boards without a claim — only a physical act would be credible.

## Mechanic
A bronze statue of a small girl, hands on hips, staring down the Charging Bull — placed on Bowling Green, lower Manhattan, on International Women's Day 2017. No copy beyond a small plaque. The statue changed the meaning of an existing landmark without touching it. Social media did the rest: the Fearless Girl became one of the most photographed spots in New York City within 24 hours.

## Why it worked
P17 (design as idea): the entire campaign is one sculpture. No copy needed. P09 (user as co-author): millions of people photographed themselves with the Fearless Girl and shared — the campaign's media was the audience's participation. The genius is the contextual dependency: the girl means something only because of the bull. Remove the bull, remove the meaning. State Street did not add a statement to a space; they changed what an existing statement meant.

## Steal
Find a symbol in the culture that carries a meaning you want to challenge. Rather than attacking it, add something to its context that changes its meaning without changing the original. The addition says everything; the original does the work.

# The Artois Probability (Stella Artois · 2024)

## Insight
Chance encounters — meeting a stranger who becomes a lifelong friend, being in the right place at the right time — feel magical but are just improbable mathematics. What if a beer brand quantified the improbability of the moments it witnesses?

## Mechanic
Stella Artois created content around the statistical probability of famous cultural moments and coincidences, framing the brand as present at improbable intersections of human experience. The absurdist precision of "calculating" the odds of serendipity is the tone.

## Why it worked
The campaign hijacks cultural moments by reframing them through the brand's lens (being there, sharing a beer). The absurdist use of statistical language on fundamentally human experiences creates an ironic distance that feels smart rather than forced.

## Steal
Find a frame that lets your brand comment on cultural moments without inserting itself awkwardly. A methodology or lens (statistical, scientific, historical) can make the brand's presence feel earned rather than opportunistic.

# Ice Skating Priests (Stella Artois · 1994)

## Insight

"Reassuringly Expensive" positions Stella as the beer worth any price or sacrifice. The humor of priests — symbols of self-denial — abandoning propriety for a pint of Stella becomes a compressed argument: even those sworn to abstinence cannot resist.

## Mechanic

A TV film featuring Catholic priests discovering the pleasure of ice skating while presumably fetching Stella Artois. The comedy lies in watching authority figures behave with undignified joy. Part of a long-running UK campaign series that built Stella's premium positioning through absurdist vignettes.

## Why it worked

P11 (absurd as carrier): the priests-on-ice premise bypasses the viewer's resistance to alcohol advertising through pure comic incongruity. P16 (long-form drama) builds the joke slowly enough that the payoff earns its space. The "Reassuringly Expensive" platform proved that premium positioning could be delivered entirely through tone rather than product claims.

## Steal

When the product claim is a price premium, demonstrate that premium through the quality and irreverence of the storytelling — not through aspirational lifestyle imagery. Make the joke expensive.
