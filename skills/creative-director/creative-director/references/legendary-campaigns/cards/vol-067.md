# Follow the Arches: Year 1 (McDonald's · 2019)

## Insight

The Follow the Arches design language — logo fragment as directional tool — can extend into a serialized system, with each year adding new applications of the same foundational visual idea.

## Mechanic

Year 1 extension of the Follow the Arches platform: new OOH applications using fragmented arches as navigation and wayfinding tools, building the system across more markets and contexts.

## Why it worked

Serialization of a design idea: once the visual language was proven in Year 0, the system could expand. Each new execution reinforced the brand confidence argument without repeating it.

## Steal

A strong design idea should have years of executions available, not just one campaign burst. Build the system first; the executions follow.

# Follow the Arches: Year 2 (McDonald's · 2020)

## Insight

The Follow the Arches visual language continues to expand — demonstrating that a strong design idea is a platform, not a campaign, compounding brand recognition over time.

## Mechanic

Second year of the Follow the Arches design system: further OOH applications with fragmented arches adapted to new contexts, locations, and navigation scenarios, deepening the visual language.

## Why it worked

Serialization and ritual: returning to the same design system annually built audience familiarity and anticipation. Each year's execution reinforced the brand confidence that animated Year 0.

## Steal

The best design ideas don't end after one campaign — they become the brand's visual vocabulary. Invest in systems that compound rather than reset.

# The Showdown (McDonald's · 1993)

## Insight

In 1993, Larry Bird and Michael Jordan were the two most famous athletes in America, and their basketball skills were at the peak of their cultural power. The question: what if they competed not in a game, but in a shooting challenge where the prize was a McDonald's meal? The premise inverts celebrity gravity — these men, worth tens of millions, care intensely about getting a Big Mac.

## Mechanic

A Super Bowl TV spot: Larry Bird and Michael Jordan play a game of H-O-R-S-E for a McDonald's meal, with increasingly impossible shots — "off the scoreboard, off the ceiling, nothing but net." The shots escalate to complete absurdity while both players maintain absolute earnestness. The film ends before revealing the winner. A sequel (on TV) revealed the result. The commercial became one of the most remembered ads of the year.

## Why it worked

P11 (absurd as carrier): two immortals of basketball competing with deadly seriousness over fast food is pure comic gold — the stakes mismatch is the premise. The escalating impossibility of the shots mirrors the escalating absurdity of the situation. The casting was perfect: Bird and Jordan's genuine rivalry gave the comedy a foundation of real tension that made the joke richer than pure farce.

## Steal

Invert the stakes: take your product (ordinary, everyday) and place it in the hands of people for whom nothing is ordinary. The gap between the celebrity's world and the product's humble category generates the comedy without requiring anything more than honest execution.

# Chicken (Mercedes-Benz · 2013)

## Insight
Magic Body Control — a suspension system that keeps the car level on any terrain — is an invisible technology. The smoother the ride, the less you notice it. Making invisibility visible requires an analogy everyone can instantly understand.

## Mechanic
A viral film showing chickens walking in slow motion, their heads staying perfectly still while their bodies bob with each step — demonstrating the same stabilization principle as Mercedes' Magic Body Control system. No car. No road. Just chickens, accompanied to "Heads Will Roll" by Yeah Yeah Yeahs.

## Why it worked
The chicken's natural head-stabilization reflex is a perfect, universally recognizable demonstration of the technology principle. The absurdist choice — chickens in a car ad — stops the scroll instantly. The joke lands in three seconds; the product truth is embedded in the joke. Pure P14 (product as proof) delivered through P11 (absurd carrier).

## Steal
Find the clearest natural analogy for a technical product benefit, even if the analogy seems wildly unrelated. A perfect analogy is more credible than a perfect demo. Make the analogy the ad, not the support for the ad.
