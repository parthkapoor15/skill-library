# Recycle Me (Coca-Cola · 2022)

## Insight
Coca-Cola cans and bottles are among the most recognised objects on earth. Their iconic shapes are so familiar they're legible even when crushed, crumpled, or deformed. That deformation — the evidence of use — is also the beginning of recycling.

## Mechanic
Ogilvy created OOH executions showing crushed, crumpled, and deformed Coca-Cola cans and bottles — still recognisably Coca-Cola — with the simple message "Recycle Me." The deformation, normally a sign of waste, becomes a demonstration that the product's identity survives destruction and can be reborn.

## Why it worked
The design insight is the idea: the brand is so strong it survives its own destruction. This makes the call-to-action (recycle) feel positive rather than guilty — the can wants to be recycled, not thrown away. P17 (Design as Idea) because the crushed form IS the message; P04 (Brand as Activist) because the brand incurs no benefit by asking consumers to recycle instead of buy.

## Steal
Take the most unflattering visual state of your product — used, broken, empty — and ask what message it communicates if you treat it as the hero rather than hiding it.

# Small World Machines (Coca-Cola · 2012)

## Insight
India and Pakistan have one of the world's most charged political relationships — yet ordinary Indians and Pakistanis share more cultural DNA than any border acknowledges. Coca-Cola's claim ("Open Happiness") requires demonstrating that shared humanity is possible across the deepest cultural divisions.

## Mechanic
Vending machines equipped with two-way live video screens were placed in a shopping mall in Lahore and a mall in Delhi. When two strangers from each country approached simultaneously, they could only get a Coke if they completed a task together — touching hands, drawing peace signs, dancing. Real people, real moment, filmed as a social experiment.

## Why it worked
The mechanic forced co-operation at the most intimate scale — two strangers, face to face, across a political divide — and made it joyful rather than solemn. The social experiment yielded genuine moments of connection that no scripted version could replicate. Coca-Cola was the enabler, not the hero.

## Steal
When a brand claim is about connection, the campaign must produce real connection rather than depicting it. Build the mechanism, step back, and document what happens. The brand's job is infrastructure, not performance.

# Video Game (Coca-Cola · 2013)

## Insight
Video games are often framed as antisocial, isolating activities. But gaming is genuinely social for millions of people — it's how friendships are maintained, how people connect across distance. The social dimension of gaming is invisible in advertising.

## Mechanic
A film set in a video game world that shows characters from the game having a genuinely human, friendship-centered experience — reframing the gaming context as a space of real connection rather than escape. Coca-Cola appears as the connective element.

## Why it worked
By taking the video game world seriously as a social space rather than treating it as a gimmick or an addiction metaphor, the spot speaks to gamers on their own terms. Coca-Cola's connection platform extends naturally into digital-social spaces without condescending to the audience.

## Steal
When entering a new cultural context (gaming, esports, streaming), don't interpret the culture from the outside. Understand the genuine social function it serves for its participants and address that function directly.

# Man-Mom (Combos · 2008)

## Insight
Combos positioned itself as "what your mom would feed you if your mom were a man" — a deliberately absurd framing of masculine snack culture. The insight: men's food choices are quietly policed by gender norms, and a snack brand that lampoons that policing earns affection.

## Mechanic
A campaign built around the "Man-Mom" character — an absurdist mashup that embodied the brand's self-aware macho positioning. The creative committed to the bit: Combos as the snack endorsed by an impossible figure.

## Why it worked
Absurdist masculinity humor in food advertising is well-worn territory, but the "Man-Mom" premise was specific enough to stand out. The self-aware quality (the campaign knows it's being ridiculous about masculinity) creates the ironic_sincerity that makes P11 work at its best.

## Steal
Find the absurd cultural contradiction at the heart of your category's positioning and make it the character. Don't resolve the contradiction — inhabit it.

# Rabbit (Comcast · 2009)

## Insight
Internet speed advertising is uniformly earnest and claim-heavy. A telecom brand willing to be genuinely funny in an absurd register stands out by default in a category that takes itself too seriously.

## Mechanic
A film featuring a rabbit in an inexplicably domestic situation related to internet speed and entertainment — pure absurdist premise executed with deadpan seriousness. The D&AD award suggests craft execution above category norms.

## Why it worked
In a category where every competitor speaks with the same serious, claim-forward voice, an absurdist departure with genuine craft execution is immediately memorable. The rabbit becomes the brand's permission to not be boring about internet speeds.

## Steal
In commoditised categories where every brand sounds the same, tonal differentiation alone can be a competitive advantage. You don't need a better claim — you need a different register.
