# Words Hurt (Progressive · 2012)

## Insight
Insurance industry jargon — "deductible," "premium," "co-pay," "exclusion" — causes genuine pain to people who don't understand it and can't afford not to. Making that pain literal and visible turns a dry frustration into shareable comedy.

## Mechanic
A film featuring characters being physically struck or wounded by insurance terminology — visual literalism applied to the painful language of the category. The comedic register frames Progressive as the brand that makes insurance understandable.

## Why it worked
Turning abstract consumer frustration (confusing jargon) into physical comedy creates shareability while communicating a product benefit (clarity). The absurdism was grounded in a real and universal consumer pain.

## Steal
Consumer frustrations that are real but invisible (jargon, complexity, hidden costs) become shareable when literalised. Find the abstract pain and make it physical — the externalisation is both the joke and the insight.

# Stratos (Red Bull · 2012)

## Insight
"Gives You Wings" is not a slogan — it is a physics claim. If Red Bull gives you wings, the logical endpoint is not flying — it is jumping from the edge of space. Every word of the brand promise was in the event.

## Mechanic
Felix Baumgartner ascended to 128,100 feet (39 kilometres) in a helium balloon and jumped, breaking the sound barrier in freefall. The jump was live-streamed on YouTube — 8 million simultaneous viewers, the largest live stream in YouTube history at that point. Red Bull's branding was on the suit, the capsule, and every frame of footage. No ad was necessary; the event was the ad.

## Why it worked
Stratos was not a sponsored event — it was a Red Bull production from conception to execution. The brand was the protagonist. "Gives You Wings" was proven at its most literal and extreme possible interpretation. The live stream transformed passive viewership into shared global experience: watching something that might kill someone, in real time, with everyone else.

## Steal
If your brand promise can be expressed as a physical act, find the most extreme version of that act and make it real. Don't sponsor it — own it. The difference between sponsoring an event and owning an event is the difference between a logo placement and a brand definition.

# Escape the Sofa (Reebok · 2004)

## Insight
Most people who buy athletic footwear are not athletes — they are people who aspire to move more but routinely choose comfort over activity. "Escape the Sofa" acknowledged the real enemy: not competitors but the gravitational pull of inertia and comfort.

## Mechanic
A film using absurdist humor (P11) to animate the sofa as an antagonist — a sentient force of comfort that fights back against people who try to leave it and exercise. Long-form storytelling (P16) gives the conflict time to develop.

## Why it worked
Naming the real enemy (P02 element within P11): not "buy our shoes" but "your sofa is conspiring against you." The absurdism makes the enemy playful rather than shameful, which lowers the psychological barrier to action. Reebok positioned itself as the solution to a universal problem, not a premium product for serious athletes.

## Steal
In a category where the real barrier is psychological inertia, make inertia the villain — and give it a character. The audience laughs at the villain because they recognize it, and in laughing, they create distance from it.

# Terry Tate: Office Linebacker (Reebok · 2003)

## Insight
Office life is full of petty violations — leaving food in the break room, not replacing the paper in the printer, scheduling unnecessary meetings. Everyone fantasizes about consequences. The insight: what if there were a linebacker who physically tackled office violators with NFL-level force?

## Mechanic
A series of short films featuring Terry Tate, a professional linebacker who works in an office. When colleagues commit minor workplace infractions, Tate tackles them with full force through office furniture, trash cans, and colleagues, shouting extended rants about their violations. Shot in a realistic office setting with real-looking documentary-style camera work. Aired in Super Bowl 2003 and became one of the first pre-YouTube viral videos.

## Why it worked
P11 (absurd as carrier): the physical incongruity — NFL aggression in an office setting — is the entire joke, and the joke never stops being funny because the violence is real-looking even as the premise is impossible. P16 (long-form drama): the films work as short character pieces — Tate has a personality, a relationship with the office, a code of conduct. Reebok became associated with athletic performance intensity applied to a comic civilian context.

## Steal
Place a character with extreme specialized competence in a context where that competence is wildly inappropriate. The comedy comes from the competence being deployed with full seriousness — Tate does not think his tacklings are funny.

# Refugee Nation (2016)

## Insight
At the 2016 Rio Olympics, 65 million displaced people had no flag to carry, no anthem to stand for, no nation to represent. The symbolic exclusion from a ceremony built on national identity made their statelessness visible in the most globally-watched event on earth.

## Mechanic
A flag was designed for the Refugee Olympic Team — its pattern derived from the orange of life jackets worn by people crossing the Mediterranean. An anthem was composed by a Syrian composer using music he made as a refugee. Both were submitted for official use at the Games.

## Why it worked
The design decisions (flag, anthem) are the activism. The orange is not a metaphor; it is the literal material of survival. P17 (Design as Idea) and P04 (Brand as Activist) combine: the design IS the statement, and creating it publicly is the action. No slogan required.

## Steal
When your cause involves a group that has been symbolically excluded, find the symbol they're excluded from (flag, anthem, name) and design the version they deserve. Make the design the campaign.
