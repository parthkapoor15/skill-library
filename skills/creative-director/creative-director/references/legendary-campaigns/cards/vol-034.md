# Wasted (E*Trade · 2008)

## Insight
Paying a financial advisor feels prudent. Realising you could have managed your own investments for a fraction of the cost — with the same or better results — feels like having been conned. The money spent on advisors was "wasted" in the most embarrassing sense.

## Mechanic
Super Bowl spot: a baby, sitting in a home office, talks directly to camera about online investing — and the idiocy of paying someone else to do it. "I just used two-thirds of my diaper budget." The baby is more competent at this than most adults. E*Trade's self-service investing platform endorsed by the youngest possible expert.

## Why it worked
The talking baby externalised the insight (this is so simple even a baby could do it) without stating it directly. The absurdity was the argument. The campaign ran for years, with the baby growing up and becoming a cultural figure.

## Steal
When the product benefit is radical simplicity, find the most extreme version of a naive user who can still operate it effortlessly. The naivety makes the simplicity claim; the execution makes it funny.

# Baby & Me (Evian · 2013)

## Insight
The third iteration of Evian's "live young" baby series required a new mechanic while remaining true to the platform. The insight: the relationship between an adult and their inner baby is not just reflection — it is interaction. They can dance together.

## Mechanic
A film in which adults encounter their baby selves — in reflections, on the other side of windows — and begin a synchronized dance sequence. Set to Ini Kamoze's "Here Comes the Hotstepper" (remixed). The baby movements match the adults' movements, creating a mirror dance between present self and younger self. 60 million YouTube views in two weeks.

## Why it worked
P11 (absurd as carrier): the synchronized baby dance escalates from cute to euphoric without losing the brand idea. P16 (long-form drama): the emotional arc builds — a single moment of wonder becomes a city-wide dance sequence. This was Evian's most shared spot and demonstrated that the "live young" platform could sustain three iterations without diminishing returns when each iteration found a genuinely new expression.

## Steal
A strong platform can sustain multiple campaigns if each execution finds a new emotional angle on the same insight. Baby & Me is not Roller Babies with a new song — it adds the concept of relationship (the adult and baby dancing together) that was absent from the earlier spots.

# Baby Inside (Evian · 2011)

## Insight
The Evian "live young" platform established that youth is internal — a state of mind and body rather than an age. The logical extreme: every adult has a baby inside them, waiting to come out when they drink Evian.

## Mechanic
A spot where adults going about their daily lives catch their reflections — but the reflections show babies performing the same actions. A businessman catches his baby reflection dancing. The premise is played straight, not for laughs. Part of the Evian "live young" ongoing series following Roller Babies.

## Why it worked
P11 (absurd as carrier): the absurdism is gentler here than Roller Babies — more conceptual than spectacular. The conceit (baby reflections) is simple enough to execute without CGI complexity. It extends the Roller Babies brand idea in a more intimate, personal register, turning the campaign from spectacle into insight.

## Steal
After establishing a spectacular campaign, the follow-up can afford to be more intimate. The audience already knows the brand's absurdist mode; the next iteration can demonstrate the idea's depth rather than its scale.

# Roller Babies (Evian · 2009)

## Insight
Evian's positioning around youth and vitality had been visual for decades — young, fit bodies in natural settings. The insight is that the most literal expression of "live young" is babies: the ultimate symbol of new life, unmarked by time. But babies doing adult things is inherently absurd and delightful, which makes the brand's serious claim memorable through pure spectacle.

## Mechanic
A film showing CGI babies rollerblading to hip-hop music — breakdancing, doing tricks, forming human pyramids. Technically, the film is a marvel: babies are motion-captured and digitally composited performing adult athletic moves. The song "Rapper's Delight" by Sugarhill Gang. No voiceover. No claim. Just babies on skates. Set a Guinness World Record for most-viewed online ad at the time with 45 million YouTube views.

## Why it worked
P16 (long-form drama) in a non-narrative sense: the film sustains interest through escalating spectacle. P11 (absurd as carrier): the absurdity of skating babies is so committed and technically accomplished that it becomes something else — a kind of wonder. The brand link ("live young") is logical without being stated. The CGI quality was genuinely unprecedented for advertising at the time.

## Steal
Viral mechanics require an image that cannot be explained without sharing it. A description of "babies rollerblading" makes no sense; the experience of watching it is irresistible. Design for the description gap — if you can convey it fully in words, it will not be shared.

# Expensify This (Expensify · 2018)

## Insight

Expense reporting is everyone's least favorite work task — even rockstars have to do it. Expensify is the brand that acknowledges the absurdity of the category.

## Mechanic

Music video with rapper 2 Chainz and Lil Dicky celebrating the glamour of expensing things, turning the mundane act of submitting receipts into an aspirational lifestyle statement via full hip-hop production.

## Why it worked

Absurdism as carrier: by taking the category's most boring feature (receipt submission) and staging it in the genre least associated with accounting, the contrast became the joke and the brand became memorable in a category where no one has a personality.

## Steal

Find the most boring function of your product. Ask what genre of culture could not be further from that function. Collide them completely. The gap is the humor.
