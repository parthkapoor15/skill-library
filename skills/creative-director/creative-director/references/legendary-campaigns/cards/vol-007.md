# Unlock (Apple · 2018)

## Insight

Technology should remove friction from moments that matter — not add it. The iPhone is a key that opens possibility rather than a device that complicates access.

## Mechanic

Short film showing how Face ID and iPhone features unlock real-life moments — presented as a series of human scenarios where the phone removes barriers and opens doors, literal and metaphorical.

## Why it worked

Long-form drama applied to product: Apple's extended film format let the metaphor of "unlocking" breathe across multiple story beats, making a functional feature feel emotionally meaningful.

## Steal

Take a product's most utilitarian function. Find the human metaphor that underlies it. Build a film around the metaphor rather than the feature.

# Van Gogh BNB (Art Institute of Chicago · 2016)

## Insight
Van Gogh's bedroom paintings are among the most reproduced images in art history. Everyone knows what the room looks like. What would it feel like to actually sleep in it?

## Mechanic
The Art Institute of Chicago partnered with Airbnb to recreate Van Gogh's bedroom at 1:1 scale as a bookable accommodation. Guests could spend the night in the exact room depicted in the paintings, with the artworks visible in the museum during the day.

## Why it worked
The leap from passive viewing to overnight experience collapses the distance between audience and art in the most literal way possible. The Airbnb platform provides the booking infrastructure; the design precision (matching every element of the painting) is the craft. The activation generated global media at the cost of a room renovation.

## Steal
Find the iconic image from your brand's world that people know but have never inhabited. Build the room. Let people sleep in it.

# Cinema (AT&T · 2019)

## Insight
Connection — AT&T's product category — is most meaningful in the moments where two people are brought together in ways that would otherwise be impossible. Cinema, as a shared collective experience, is one of the few places where strangers still sit together in the dark and feel the same thing at the same time.

## Mechanic
A long-form film using cinema as a metaphor for human connection — the shared emotional experience of a film in a theatre as a demonstration of what genuine connection feels like. High production, cinematic quality that was designed to be shown in the cinema context it depicted.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16) allowed AT&T to move away from telecom category communication (speed, coverage, price) and occupy a cultural space around connection as an emotional rather than a technical concept. The format matched the subject — a cinema ad about cinema.

## Steal
When a brand's core concept (connection, communication, movement) can be demonstrated through a cultural form rather than a product demonstration, use the cultural form. The medium proves the message without saying it.

# Close to Home (AT&T · 2018)

## Insight
Texting while driving kills 3,477 people annually in the US. The abstract statistic doesn't move people — but the specific, named human being who died because someone couldn't wait does.

## Mechanic
A long-form documentary-style film series featuring real victims of distracted driving — and the drivers who caused the accidents. Both sides share their stories without dramatization: the person who died, the person who survived them, the driver who lives with what they did.

## Why it worked
AT&T had run distracted driving campaigns for years. This iteration moved from statistics and reconstructed scenarios to real people in real aftermath. Long-form drama (P16) gave enough time for guilt, grief, and responsibility to accumulate without simplification. Truth-telling (P13): showing the driver as a person who made an ordinary mistake with irreversible consequences was more disturbing than any villain framing.

## Steal
In behaviour change campaigns, guilt is more powerful when it's specific and human than when it's abstract and statistical. Showing the person whose text could not wait — and what happened — creates a mirror the audience cannot dismiss.

# We Try Harder (Avis · 1962)

## Insight

In 1962, Avis was a perennial #2 to Hertz in car rental and had been losing money for 13 consecutive years. Bill Bernbach's insight was counterintuitive: don't try to out-Hertz Hertz — acknowledge being #2 and make it the most powerful possible brand position. If you're #2, you try harder. If you try harder, you give better service. The limitation becomes the advantage.

## Mechanic

A print campaign: stark layouts, dense copy, direct address. "Avis is only No. 2 in rent a cars. So why go with us? We try harder." The campaigns continued: "Avis can't afford dirty ashtrays." "When you're not the biggest, you have to be the best." Every execution reinforced the #2 position as a service promise. Avis went from losing $3.2 million to profiting $1.2 million in the campaign's first year. The campaign ran for 50 years.

## Why it worked

P06 (limitation as power): acknowledging weakness preempted every competitor attack — Hertz could not attack Avis for being #2 when Avis was already saying it. P02 (enemy or conflict): Hertz is the implicit enemy, never named, always present. P17 (design as idea): DDB's spare, word-heavy layouts communicated through their tone — intelligent, direct, confident — that this was a brand that respected its audience's intelligence. No photography, no lifestyle imagery — just honest language in clean typography.

## Steal

The moment a brand admits its actual position is the moment it earns the right to be believed about everything else it claims. Honesty about weakness is more persuasive than any claim of strength, because the audience already knows the weakness and is waiting to see if the brand will acknowledge it.
