# The Best Job in the World (Tourism Queensland · 2009)

## Insight
Queensland's tourism board had a small budget but needed global coverage. The insight: if they created a genuine job opportunity — one that was objectively the best job in the world — the world's media would cover it for free. The product (the islands of Queensland) would become the prize rather than the subject of advertising.

## Mechanic
A real job listing for "Island Caretaker" of Hamilton Island, Great Barrier Reef: six months, AU$150,000, a luxury villa, and the responsibility of exploring the islands and blogging about it. The job was advertised through a single newspaper ad in 11 countries. The global response was 34,000 video applications from 200 countries. Every major news outlet covered it. The successful candidate generated 6 months of authentic content. Total advertising spend: AU$1.2 million. Earned media value: AU$400+ million.

## Why it worked
P09 (user as co-author) at scale: 34,000 applicants created campaign content, each sharing their video application. P04 (brand as activist) implicit: Tourism Queensland made a real commitment (a real salary, a real job, a real villa) which gave the stunt credibility. The mechanic was perfect — a genuine opportunity that could only exist in Queensland, demonstrating the destination through the act of creating the job rather than through advertising the destination. Every journalist who wrote "would you apply for this job?" was writing a Queensland tourism ad.

## Steal
When your product is genuinely desirable, the most powerful campaign is not advertising the product but creating an opportunity that proves it. Make the product the prize and let the audience compete for it. The competition becomes the campaign.

# Body Bags (Truth · 1998)

## Insight
Tobacco companies knew their product killed people and hid it. Young people were angry, but had no visceral, undeniable proof delivered at scale. Telling teens "smoking kills" changed nothing — showing who killed whom could.

## Mechanic
Youth activists delivered 1,200 body bags to the headquarters of Philip Morris in New York City — one for each person who dies daily from tobacco-related illness in the United States. The stunt was filmed, amplified through TV spots, and became the launch of the Truth anti-tobacco campaign that would define a generation.

## Why it worked
The body bags made an abstract statistic physical and unmovable. Philip Morris could not spin 1,200 bags on their doorstep. The action gave young people agency — they were the activists, not the victims. Truth inverted the power dynamic: instead of vulnerable teens needing protection, teens were confronting a corporation. P17 element: the visual language of the bags was design-as-message — no headline needed.

## Steal
When a statistic has been cited so often it no longer lands — make it physical and unmovable. Body count = body bags. Abstract death toll = concrete objects delivered to the responsible party. The action forces a confrontation the villain cannot walk away from.

# Interface Interruption (Tubi · 2023)

## Insight
The Super Bowl ad break is the most jealously guarded viewing ritual in American culture — nobody touches the remote. Tubi, a free streaming challenger, couldn't buy the same ad volume as established competitors. So they made everyone else's ad time work for them.

## Mechanic
During the Super Bowl broadcast, Tubi ran a 15-second spot that precisely mimicked the UI of a Tubi app accidentally opening on a TV — showing the streaming interface appearing to "take over" the broadcast. Millions of viewers thought someone had grabbed the remote. Social media exploded.

## Why it worked
Format as idea (P01): the UI simulation was only possible because Tubi understood exactly how its own interface would read as a disruption on a living-room television. Absurdism (P11): the premise was technically absurd but executed with enough visual fidelity to create genuine confusion. The mechanic cost a fraction of a typical Super Bowl spot and generated coverage equivalent to a much larger media buy.

## Steal
Instead of competing for attention in a crowded format, attack the ritual surrounding the format. The Super Bowl remote-touching taboo was the free asset — Tubi just needed to trigger it.

# The First Digital Nation (Tuvalu Government · 2022)

## Insight
Tuvalu, a Pacific island nation, is being submerged by rising seas driven by climate change — its physical territory will cease to exist within decades. But climate rhetoric had become so saturated that a new drowning nation was no longer news. The question was how to reframe the crisis as a legal and sovereign innovation rather than another catastrophe.

## Mechanic
At COP27, Tuvalu's Minister for Justice, Communication and Foreign Affairs delivered a speech standing knee-deep in seawater, announcing that Tuvalu would become the world's first digital nation — recreating itself as a sovereign state in the metaverse, preserving its legal personhood, cultural heritage, and government functions even after the physical land is gone.

## Why it worked
Climate fatigue had made emotional appeals ineffective. By reframing the crisis as a legal precedent — a genuinely novel question of international law — the campaign earned coverage across news, technology, legal, and diplomatic outlets simultaneously. Tech as canvas (P18): the metaverse wasn't decoration but structural solution. Brand as activist (P04) at a state level: real sovereign action.

## Steal
When an issue has been communicated emotionally to exhaustion, switch frames to the institutional or legal dimension. "What unprecedented legal action does this crisis require?" is more newsworthy than "this crisis is terrible." Novelty of solution beats amplification of problem.

# What's Happening? (Twitter · 2015)

## Insight
Twitter's core mechanic — asking "what's happening?" — was its greatest asset, but the brand had failed to communicate why answering that question in public matters. Real-time cultural conversation was happening; Twitter needed to own it.

## Mechanic
Integrated campaign positioning Twitter as the live conversation layer of the world — news, sports, politics, entertainment — centering on the platform's "What's Happening?" prompt as the axis of participation.

## Why it worked
User-as-co-author (P09): every tweet is a contribution to a collective live narrative. The campaign made Twitter's participatory nature explicit rather than functional. Positioning around the verb (happening, live, now) differentiated from static social feeds.

## Steal
When your product is a verb, make the verb the campaign. "What's happening?" is better than any brand promise. The question implicates the user — it cannot be answered without participation.
