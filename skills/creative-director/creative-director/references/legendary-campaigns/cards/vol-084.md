# Adoptable (Pedigree · 2020)

## Insight
Millions of dogs need adoption, but most people find shelter dogs through a search process that feels clinical and sad — opposed to the joyful, browse-and-discover behaviour they already have on Instagram.

## Mechanic
Pedigree built a feature using Instagram's native dog face filter: when the filter detected a dog's face, it would automatically overlay adoptable shelter dogs from the local area — photos sourced from real shelters — and enable immediate enquiry. The technology (P18) ran through the platform users already used to browse dogs.

## Why it worked
Technology as canvas (P18): the dog-detection filter was not a gimmick but the core mechanism — it inserted adoptable dogs into the discovery behaviour already happening. User as co-author (P09): engagement with the feature generated shelter visibility without paid media. Pedigree's brand purpose (supporting dogs) was enacted, not stated.

## Steal
Map the actual discovery behaviour of your audience, then build a feature that serves the brand purpose inside that existing behaviour rather than interrupting it. The platform constraint becomes the product feature.

# Child Replacement Programme (Pedigree · 2016)

## Insight
When children leave home, many parents experience a gap in caregiving that they are emotionally unprepared for. Pedigree noticed that dog adoption rates spike significantly when children leave — a correlation that pointed to a real human need that no brand had addressed directly.

## Mechanic
An integrated campaign that openly marketed Pedigree dog adoption to "empty nesters" — parents whose children had recently left home — with the absurdist framing of literally replacing the child with a dog. Materials were crafted in the genre of family-planning communications.

## Why it worked
Behaviour inversion (P03): dog food brands talk about dogs; this campaign talked about a human emotional transition, with the dog as the solution. Absurdism (P11): the framing as a formal "programme" with clinical-style materials made the joke land while making the genuine insight feel sharper.

## Steal
Find the unspoken emotional context behind a purchase decision — the human situation the product actually resolves — and market to that situation rather than the product category. The category code becomes a supporting character.

# K9FM (Pedigree · 2012)

## Insight
Shelter dogs spend most of their time alone in kennels, with no stimulation. Research suggested that music reduces stress in dogs. What if there was a radio station playing music specifically composed and selected for the auditory range and stress patterns of dogs?

## Mechanic
Pedigree launched K9FM — a 24/7 radio station broadcasting exclusively for dogs at shelters. Music was purpose-composed at frequencies and rhythms scientifically calibrated for canine anxiety. The absurdist premise (a radio station with no human audience) generated enormous PR; the underlying welfare intervention was genuinely real.

## Why it worked
Absurdism (P11): the premise is inherently ridiculous — a radio station nobody listens to — but the execution was rigorous and caring. The joke and the genuine welfare intervention operated simultaneously, which made the campaign feel warm rather than cynical.

## Steal
Absurdist premises that contain a sincere act of care are more powerful than purely comedic ones. The audience laughs and then feels something — the humour is the delivery mechanism for the sincerity, not a substitute for it.

# Price Packs (PENNY · 2025)

## Insight
Discount retail faces a communications paradox: communicate low prices without communicating low quality. Design-led communication (P17) can signal quality while the mechanic communicates value.

## Mechanic
An integrated campaign combining participatory mechanics (P09) with design-led communication (P17) — using the price packs themselves as the medium. The packaging design carries the campaign message, making the product's value proposition a design statement.

## Why it worked
Retail advertising commonly shouts prices; PENNY used design to whisper them. When the pack becomes the communication, the product is its own media channel. P09 adds participatory momentum: customers engaging with or sharing the packs.

## Steal
Turn your lowest-cost packaging into your highest-quality brand communication. The constraint of affordability becomes the canvas for design intelligence.

# Cindy Crawford (Pepsi · 1992)

## Insight

Cindy Crawford in 1992 was the world's most famous supermodel. Two young boys watching her walk to a vending machine are not watching a model — they are watching the most desirable person they could imagine. But then she drinks the Pepsi. The boys' expressions make clear: they are admiring the can, not the woman. The joke inverts the expected sexual economy of celebrity advertising.

## Mechanic

A Super Bowl TV spot: two young boys sitting on a fence outside a country store watch Cindy Crawford arrive in a car, walk to a Pepsi machine, and drink a Pepsi in slow motion. Slow motion, iconic entrance, typically aspirational direction. Then the reveal: cut to the boys' faces, one saying "Is that a great car or what?" The other: admiring the can. The spot subverted celebrity endorsement by making the joke at its own expense.

## Why it worked

P16 (long-form drama) used the full build of classic celebrity entrance — slow motion, music, admiring gaze — to set up the inversion. The spot was self-aware about its own mechanisms: it knew the audience expected a certain kind of male gaze and subverted it for comedy. The irony communicated brand confidence — Pepsi was secure enough to make fun of its own marketing conventions. The execution became a touchstone for Super Bowl advertising craft.

## Steal

If you're going to use the conventions of aspirational celebrity advertising, go all the way in — then subvert them in the final beat. The subversion requires the full investment in the convention to earn its payoff. Half-measures make it merely confusing.
