# The Cog (Honda · 2003)

## Insight
Engineering precision is not a functional claim — it is a philosophy. The audience does not want to be told Honda builds reliable cars; they want to feel the obsessive care that goes into every component. The Rube Goldberg machine does not describe the Accord; it embodies the logic of a car where everything works in relation to everything else.

## Mechanic
A two-minute film showing a chain-reaction machine built entirely from Accord components — windshield wipers, exhaust pipes, tyres, seats — triggering each other in sequence across a clean white space. 606 takes over four months to achieve a single working sequence. No CGI. No editing tricks. The final product: a car rolling off an incline as the tagline appears: "Isn't it nice when things just work?"

## Why it worked
P14 (product as proof): every component working in the film is a Honda component, so the demonstration is literally about Honda quality. P08 (craft as message): 606 attempts over four months transfers the obsessive precision of that process to the brand's engineering culture. The 30-second cut for TV could not contain it — audiences sought out the two-minute version online in 2003, years before YouTube. It won the Grand Prix at Cannes and spawned an industry conversation about whether the chain reaction was faked (it was not).

## Steal
Choose a demonstration format where the effort of production is inseparable from the product claim. If the making of the ad requires the same precision as the making of the product, the ad is self-evidencing. The behind-the-scenes story becomes as valuable as the film itself.

# The Other Side (Honda · 2015)

## Insight
Most people drive two versions of themselves: the responsible daily driver and the driver who secretly wants more. The Civic Type R exists for the second driver — but Honda had to prove it without alienating the first.

## Mechanic
A dual-narrative film where pressing the letter "R" on the keyboard instantly switches between two parallel lives: a mild-mannered family man in a standard Civic, and the same driver as a criminal getaway driver in a Type R. The films run simultaneously; the viewer controls which story they're in.

## Why it worked
P03 (behavior inversion): Honda — the most sensible car brand — built an ad that showed its product in a getaway from the police, told as a crime thriller. P16 gave the narrative enough cinematic quality to make both stories genuinely gripping. The interactive mechanic (pressing "R") was not a gimmick but the idea itself: the choice between the two lives is exactly the emotional territory the Type R occupies. This is one of the few interactive automotive ads that earned the format.

## Steal
When a product has a dual personality — practical and thrilling — don't pick one. Build a mechanic that lets the audience inhabit both simultaneously. The choice itself is the insight.

# This is Wholesome (Honey Maid · 2014)

## Insight
"Wholesome" had been co-opted by a narrow, nostalgic image of the American family that excluded most actual families. Reclaiming that word for real families — interracial, same-sex, single-parent — was both commercially smart and culturally necessary.

## Mechanic
A TV film featuring non-traditional American families — a same-sex couple with children, an interracial family, a tattooed father — with the tagline "This is Wholesome." When the ad drew backlash, Droga5 printed every negative comment and used them to build a sculpture spelling "Love." The response to the backlash became a second campaign more viral than the first.

## Why it worked
The original campaign was brave. The response to backlash was masterful. By making the negative comments visible and physically transforming them into their opposite, Honey Maid demonstrated its position under fire — which is when brand values actually mean something.

## Steal
A brand's response to backlash can be more defining than its original campaign. Design the counter-move before launch: if the work attracts controversy, what does an on-brand response look like that proves the point rather than retreats from it?

# Salla 2032 (House of Lapland · 2021)

## Insight
Salla is one of the coldest towns in Finland — a place no one would consider for the Summer Olympics. But by 2032, climate change will have warmed it enough to host. The absurdity of the bid was the climate warning.

## Mechanic
The town of Salla submitted a genuine-looking Olympic bid for the Summer Games 2032, complete with an IOC-style presentation video, an official application, and athlete interviews delivered in deadpan seriousness. The implicit message: "If Salla can host the Summer Olympics, the climate has already lost." Earned global press.

## Why it worked
Brand as activist (P04): the municipality put its name and credibility behind a climate message, giving it institutional weight. User as co-author (P09): the bid format invited global media and audiences to participate by taking it seriously or sharing it. The joke and the argument are the same thing — you cannot explain the absurdity without explaining climate change.

## Steal
Use a serious institutional format (Olympic bid, UN petition, patent application) to deliver a serious argument through apparent absurdity. The format provides credibility; the gap between the form and the content is the idea. The press will explain the joke, and in doing so, spread the message.

# Happy Birthday From Earth (Husqvarna · 2023)

## Insight
Garden tools are bought for the garden, but the garden's relationship to the planet is rarely made explicit. Earth Day is the annual moment when that connection surfaces — but most brands use it for corporate messaging, not for inviting real participation.

## Mechanic
An integrated campaign combining participatory elements (P09) with a seasonal ritual structure (P10) — building a collective "birthday gift" to Earth using the audience's own outdoor spaces and Husqvarna's products as the medium. User-generated green action created the campaign content.

## Why it worked
Husqvarna's core product (outdoor power tools for gardens and lawns) connects directly to the planet's green space. P09 and P10 together create a repeatable annual ritual: people are invited to participate with what they already own, and the campaign grows with each year's edition.

## Steal
When your product literally touches the natural world, use that physical connection as proof of environmental commitment — and turn it into an annual participatory act rather than a one-off statement.
