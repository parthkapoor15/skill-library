# Panyee FC (TMB Bank · 2011)

## Insight
Believing in your own potential — regardless of your starting conditions — is not natural or easy. It is a choice made despite evidence. A bank that finances small businesses understands this better than anyone.

## Mechanic
A documentary-style film telling the true story of a football team from Koh Panyee — a floating village in Thailand with no land — who built a floating football pitch from waste wood and competed in the regional championships. The film ends with a call to action about TMB Bank supporting people who dare.

## Why it worked
The story was real, which meant it required no exaggeration. The brief — a bank brief, the most generic brief in advertising — was answered with something genuinely moving because the story did the work. The brand's connection (we believe in people who try against impossible odds) was earned by the specificity of the case.

## Steal
Documentary truth in advertising is more persuasive than any fictional narrative because it cannot be dismissed as brand fantasy. Find the case study that proves your brand's point so specifically and so completely that no script could improve on it.

# Push to Add Drama (TNT · 2012)

## Insight

TNT stands for drama — but in Belgium, no one knew what TNT was. To launch the channel, the brand needed to demonstrate what drama feels like rather than explain what drama is.

## Mechanic

A "Push to Add Drama" button placed in a quiet Belgian square. One person pushes it. Orchestrated chaos erupts: ambulances, motorcycles, fighting, a woman in lingerie on a stretcher, a gang showdown — real actors, real stunts, in the actual street. The reaction of real passersby was filmed and broadcast. The button pushed the drama; the audience couldn't look away.

## Why it worked

Tech as canvas meets user as co-author: the trigger (the button) made an ordinary person the catalyst for extraordinary events — exactly what watching TNT feels like as a viewer. The stunt was the brand demonstration. The shocked faces of real people were the ratings argument. Globally viral without any paid distribution.

## Steal

If your product delivers an emotional experience, design a physical situation that delivers that exact emotion to a real person, in public, unexpectedly. Film their authentic reaction. The reaction is the ad.

# Stop Working Out in the Past (Tonal · 2025)

## Insight

Traditional gym equipment — dumbbells, barbells, weight machines — is literally designed in the past and hasn't fundamentally evolved in decades. Using old technology is working out in the past.

## Mechanic

Film using bold design and absurdist visual language to present traditional gym equipment as comically outdated — weight plates and dumbbells styled as primitive artifacts, contrasted with Tonal's modern smart-weight system as the obvious future.

## Why it worked

Design as idea meets absurdism: the concept was self-contained and visually executable without explanation. The phrase "Stop Working Out in the Past" reframed the competitive landscape — every competitor's product became evidence of the claim.

## Steal

Reframe your entire competitive set as being from the past. If your product genuinely represents a category step-change, make the obsolescence of everything else the campaign rather than listing your own features.

# With 10 Years of Life (Toshiba · 2012)

## Insight
Electronic devices are disposable by design — planned obsolescence is the industry norm. Toshiba's positioning around durability and longevity was a genuine differentiation against competitors. But "10 years" as a spec is abstract; the emotional meaning of 10 years — what changes in a life over that period — is concrete and human.

## Mechanic
A long-form film showing what 10 years of a life looks like: a character (or family) tracked through a decade of changes — relationships forming and breaking, children growing, career shifts, loss and joy. Throughout, a Toshiba device is present. The P13 truth: 10 years is not a spec; it is a significant portion of a human life, and the things that are with us for 10 years become part of our story.

## Why it worked
P16 (long-form drama): 10 years as a narrative requires the scope that only long-form allows. P13 (truth telling): the truth being told is that we measure product durability in spec numbers but experience it in life events. A 10-year device witnessed a decade of a human life — that is not a technical claim; it is a human claim.

## Steal
Convert technical specifications into their human equivalents. "10 years" = a child growing from infant to primary school; a marriage forming and maturing; a career changing course. The specification tells you nothing about why you should care; the human equivalent tells you everything.

# Dundee Returns (Tourism Australia · 2018)

## Insight
American tourists would visit Australia if they thought about it, but they don't think about it enough. The greatest Australian cultural export to America was Paul Hogan's Crocodile Dundee. What if there was a sequel?

## Mechanic
A mock Hollywood movie trailer for "Dundee: The Son of a Legend Returns Home" — starring Chris Hemsworth and Danny McBride — that ran in full before the Super Bowl, fooling many viewers into believing a new Dundee film was coming. The reveal: it was a Tourism Australia campaign. The trailer mechanic was the ideal vehicle for reaching American audiences in the Super Bowl context.

## Why it worked
Cultural hijack (P05): hijacking the Hollywood blockbuster trailer format — with genuine stars — earned attention and credibility normally unavailable to a tourist board. Absurd as carrier (P11): the joke lands precisely because Chris Hemsworth cannot play Paul Hogan's son, but plays it straight. The reveal extended the campaign's life through earned media coverage of "the hoax."

## Steal
Adopt a format that carries more cultural authority than your category normally commands — the film trailer, the tech keynote, the award acceptance speech. Authenticity of execution is everything; if the fake trailer looks like a real trailer, the joke works. If it looks like an ad, it fails.
