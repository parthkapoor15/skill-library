# McEnroe vs. McEnroe (Michelob Ultra · 2023)

## Insight

John McEnroe was one of the greatest tennis players of all time — but what would he lose to? The AI-generated version of his peak self. The question of whether peak human ability can be replicated by AI was both technically fascinating and emotionally loaded.

## Mechanic

Long-form film in which 64-year-old John McEnroe played against an AI-generated version of his 1984 peak-performance self — trained on thousands of hours of his historical match footage. Real human vs. AI human. The result was not predetermined.

## Why it worked

Tech as canvas meets long-form drama: the technology (AI coaching model playing on-screen as a younger McEnroe) was the narrative engine, not decoration. The existential question (can AI beat the person it was modeled on?) was genuinely interesting to answer. The result carried real dramatic stakes.

## Steal

When using AI in a campaign, find a question worth answering rather than a feature worth demonstrating. The best AI campaigns are experiments with uncertain outcomes — the technology earns its place only if the audience genuinely doesn't know what will happen.

# 5 O'Clock Shadow (Miller Genuine Draft · 2000)

## Insight
The moment the workday ends and working-class identity reasserts itself is a ritual unmarked by beer advertising, which typically shows the party, not the transition. The 5 o'clock shadow — the physical marker of a day's labor — is invisible in the category.

## Mechanic
An Errol Morris documentary portrait exploring masculinity, work, and the end-of-day ritual through a character whose identity is partly expressed through their 5 o'clock shadow. Part of the Miller Genuine Draft character series.

## Why it worked
The physical detail (five o'clock shadow) anchors an emotional truth about working identity and the reward of evening. Morris's observational style made the ordinary feel significant without sentimentalizing it.

## Steal
Beer's most honest advertising moment is not the celebration but the transition — the moment before the first sip, when the day ends and something shifts. That liminal moment is unowned territory in most markets.

# Duct Tape (Miller Genuine Draft · 2000)

## Insight
The pragmatic ingenuity of everyday American life — fixing things with whatever's at hand — is a cultural identity marker that beer brands almost never touch. Practicality and beer drinking coexist in the same person; the category only shows one side.

## Mechanic
An Errol Morris documentary portrait featuring a person with strong convictions about duct tape as a universal solution, delivered with the same earnestness as testimony. The Miller beer appears as ambient context in the world of the character.

## Why it worked
The character's devotion to a mundane object (duct tape) makes them eccentric and lovable simultaneously. The beer brand benefits from the association with this unpretentious, resourceful worldview — positioning MGD as the beer of people who are honest about who they are.

## Steal
Build brand character through devotion to irrelevant specifics. A character who cares enormously about something small reveals more about values than any category-standard aspiration. The specific beats the universal.

# Errol Morris Series (Miller Genuine Draft · 2000)

## Insight
Beer advertising operated with a standardized vocabulary of aspiration: sports, parties, attractive people, simple humor. Miller Genuine Draft recognized that an entire register of American life — the eccentric, the specific, the quietly odd — had been completely ignored.

## Mechanic
A multi-spot campaign commissioning documentary filmmaker Errol Morris to create a series of character portraits. Real and real-feeling Americans spoke directly to camera about their opinions, habits, and obsessions — with MGD present as ambient atmosphere rather than product hero.

## Why it worked
The campaign functions as a single advertising idea applied across many executions: "authentic American character." Morris's visual grammar (Interrotron technique, direct address) imported documentary credibility into the paid media environment. Each spot is distinct but the series builds cumulative brand personality.

## Steal
When commissioning a campaign, hire a director whose existing body of work already embodies your brand's values. The filmmaking style is part of the brief. Don't hire a documentary filmmaker and then brief them to make a commercial.

# Hot Dog (Miller Genuine Draft · 2000)

## Insight
American food identity is deeply personal and often irrational. The hot dog — emblematic of backyard culture, ballparks, summertime — carries more emotional weight than its simplicity suggests. Beer and hot dogs share a ritual context that neither exploits directly.

## Mechanic
An Errol Morris documentary portrait featuring a character with strongly held convictions about hot dogs — their preparation, their meaning, their specific requirements — delivered with full earnestness. Part of the Miller Genuine Draft Errol Morris series.

## Why it worked
The specificity of food opinion reveals character more than biography. A person with strong hot dog opinions is knowable and likable in a way that a person with generic opinions is not. The beer associates with authenticity by surrounding itself with genuinely specific people.

## Steal
Find the most specific, mundane opinion your target audience holds and make it the center of a portrait. The specificity is the charm. Any level of aspiration or abstraction kills it.
