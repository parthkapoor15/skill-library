# No Bollocks (Newcastle Brown Ale · 2014)

## Insight
Every beer brand in America spends Super Bowl money on aspirational, cinematic hokum — mountains, victory, brotherhood, freedom. Newcastle doesn't have that budget, and even if it did, it would be embarrassing to spend it on the same pantomime. The limitation is the idea.

## Mechanic
A pre-Super Bowl campaign that acknowledged, with brutal transparency, that Newcastle couldn't afford a Super Bowl ad. Fake "almost-ads" featuring Anna Kendrick discussing what their Super Bowl ad would have looked like. The campaign mocked the entire genre of Super Bowl advertising while performing in the same cultural moment. Massive earned media for zero Super Bowl spend.

## Why it worked
Absurd as carrier (P11): the joke is structural — a brand making fun of the idea of spending money on a brand campaign. Enemy or conflict (P02): the entire category of Super Bowl advertising is the enemy. Limitation as power (P06): the explicit acknowledgment of budget constraint transforms it into a creative and brand asset. Honesty so extreme it becomes charming.

## Steal
Turn your media disadvantage into your biggest media story. The brand that can't afford the table openly mocking the table is more interesting than any brand sitting at it. Radical transparency about limitations produces more trust than any aspirational claim.

# A Little Less Gravity / Defy (Nike · 2012)

## Insight
The sensation of peak athletic performance — the moment when effort disappears and movement feels effortless — is physically impossible to convey in words or through a product claim. It must be felt, not explained.

## Mechanic
A film using visual effects to create the impression of reduced gravity — athletes performing their movements with an otherworldly lightness that suggests the sensation of peak performance from inside the athlete's experience.

## Why it worked
The gravity effect externalizes an internal experience: what peak form feels like to the person inside it. This is categorically different from showing what performance looks like from the outside. The visual metaphor is simple, universally legible, and physically impossible to experience without doing what the athlete does.

## Steal
When the product delivers an internal experience (confidence, focus, physical sensation), find a visual metaphor that externalizes it. The metaphor must be immediately readable, not explained.

# Badge of Honor (Nike China · 2017)

## Insight
In China, sports injuries carry social stigma — showing physical vulnerability is culturally uncomfortable. Young Chinese athletes hide their injuries rather than wearing them as evidence of effort. Nike's insight: if you've earned the wound, it should be visible.

## Mechanic
Nike China created branded sports bandages — designed to be visible, colourful, and clearly Nike — that turned sports injuries into visible badges of athletic commitment. Participants shared their bandaged injuries on social media as proof of effort.

## Why it worked
P17 (design as idea): the product itself — a bandage — becomes the communication. The design makes the wound visible and desirable rather than hidden. P09 (user as co-author): the campaign scales through participants sharing their injuries, not through Nike paying for media. The insight is culturally specific to China (injury stigma) but the mechanic is universally legible.

## Steal
When your audience has a behaviour they hide because it's culturally uncomfortable, design something that makes hiding it more uncomfortable than showing it. The badge inverts the shame into pride through product design alone.

# Breaking2 (Nike · 2017)

## Insight
Human limits are not facts — they are narratives. The two-hour marathon had existed for decades as a story about what humans cannot do. Nike understood that attempting the impossible, not achieving it, is the more culturally resonant act.

## Mechanic
Nike recruited three elite runners — Eliud Kipchoge, Lelisa Desisa, Zersenay Tadese — to attempt a sub-two-hour marathon on a Formula 1 circuit in Monza. The attempt was live-streamed globally on Twitter. Kipchoge ran 2:00:25 — missing the record, but making it the most watched athletic attempt in history. The full documentary aired on National Geographic.

## Why it worked
P14 (product as proof) at civilisational scale: Nike's running technology is not a claim but a physical experiment with the world watching. The near-miss was narratively better than success — it kept the attempt human and honest. P16 (long-form drama) gave it a story arc that a product demo never has: tension, sacrifice, physics, failure at the edge of success. Nike did not sponsor the story; Nike was the story.

## Steal
Commission an attempt, not an ad. The process of trying, documented rigorously, is more credible than any claim. Build the story around the edge case where your product must perform at its absolute limit — the limit is where trust is earned.

# Chalkbot (Nike · 2009)

## Insight
Nike was sponsoring the Tour de France and supporting the Livestrong Foundation (Lance Armstrong era). Cancer survivors and supporters worldwide had messages of hope they wanted to share — but no way to place them on the road of the Tour de France, the world's most iconic cycling course.

## Mechanic
Deeplocal built a physical robot attached to a vehicle in the Tour de France peloton that could print chalk messages on the road in real time. People submitted messages via SMS, web, and Twitter. The robot printed them in the path of the cycling race. Messages appeared on the race road, were filmed by helicopter cameras, and were broadcast to millions of viewers worldwide. Each contributor received GPS coordinates of their printed message.

## Why it worked
P18 (tech as canvas): the technology was the idea — a robot that could transfer digital messages to the physical world's most-watched cycling stage. P09 (user as co-author): contributors generated the content and became invested in the broadcast because their specific message appeared on a global stage. The GPS coordinate follow-up personalized the scale — each person knew exactly where on the Tour their message was. The combination of physical action (chalk on road), human contribution, and global broadcast created an experience no amount of bought advertising could replicate.

## Steal
Find where digital participation can have a physical consequence visible at scale. The physical manifestation of a digital act gives it weight and permanence that purely digital campaigns lack. GPS coordinates as personalized confirmation is a model for closing the loop between mass broadcast and individual participation.
