# Evan (Sandy Hook Promise · 2016)

## Insight
The warning signs of school violence are almost always visible beforehand — but people don't know what they look like, or don't register them as significant because their attention is on something else. The audience for gun violence prevention advertising is the same audience that was already watching.

## Mechanic
A film that opens as a sweet high school romance between Evan and a mystery girl — messages written on a library desk, clues left for each other. The audience watches the love story develop over several minutes. In the final thirty seconds, the camera re-frames: the desk was also being used by another student who was leaving the actual warning signs of planned violence, visible the whole time. The audience didn't see them because they were watching the romance.

## Why it worked
The film doesn't tell the audience they might miss warning signs — it makes them experience missing warning signs. The behaviour inversion (P03) is structural: what looked like a feel-good teen romance becomes evidence of collective inattention. The truth-telling (P13) is in the "you too" implication of the final reveal: the viewer is not a bystander — they were a participant.

## Steal
Design a film in which the audience's own inattention becomes the message. They don't need to be told they miss things — if they missed the same thing in your film, the point is made more powerfully than any instruction.

# Teenage Dream (Sandy Hook Promise · 2024)

## Insight
Teenagers documenting their adolescence on phones — parties, graduations, sleepovers — and teenagers documenting active shooter incidents on the same phones, with the same trembling hands, in the same format. Both exist. Most people have seen only one.

## Mechanic
A film structured as a nostalgic teen-memory montage set to Katy Perry's "Teenage Dream" — the quintessential track of youthful optimism. As the montage progresses, the footage shifts: the joyful memories and the shooter-incident recordings become intercut, then indistinguishable. The song continues without pause.

## Why it worked
The contrast between soundtrack (euphoric) and content (terror) creates cognitive dissonance that bypasses the audience's accustomed defences to school shooting content. Behaviour inversion (P03): the format of joyful teen nostalgia is the shell for an unbearable reality. Truth-telling (P13): the footage style is consistent with what exists — these recordings are real in form even if not in specific content.

## Steal
When an audience has been numbed to the direct presentation of a horror, approach it through the aesthetic language of its opposite. Euphoric music over graphic content creates a dissonance the brain cannot smoothly process — and that discomfort carries the message.

# Most Shocking Second a Day (Save the Children · 2014)

## Insight
Syria is far away; a British child is close. The distance makes donors feel the crisis doesn't belong to them — proximity makes it impossible to ignore.

## Mechanic
A film constructed from one second of footage per day of a young British girl's life: birthday party, school, friends — then, as conflict arrives in Britain, one second per day as the world collapses around her, mirroring what Syrian children experience daily.

## Why it worked
The "second a day" format is native to the audience's social habits (a familiar creative trend at the time). Casting a relatable British child as the Syrian proxy forces empathetic projection. The structural inversion — familiar beginning, catastrophic ending — delivers impact through contrast rather than shock footage.

## Steal
Transplant a distant tragedy into the local audience's world using a single conditional proxy character. Empathy scales when the audience can see themselves in the victim.

# Climate Doctor's Certificate (School Strike 4 Climate · 2024)

## Insight
Students skipping school for climate strikes face disciplinary consequences — the system punishes the behaviour the crisis demands.

## Mechanic
Doctors (GPs) issued official medical-style certificates excusing students from school for climate strike participation, inserting the cause into a legitimate bureaucratic process that schools could not easily ignore.

## Why it worked
The campaign weaponized an official, trusted institution — the medical certificate — against the authority trying to suppress protest. It created structural legitimacy for civil disobedience.

## Steal
Embed communication inside a mandatory or trusted official process. The authority of the format (medical, legal, governmental) transfers to the message automatically.

# High School Girl (Shiseido · 2016)

## Insight

Makeup is powerful enough to transform anyone — in Japan, where beauty standards and gender conformity are particularly rigid, that transformative power is more radical than the industry typically acknowledges.

## Mechanic

Film following what appears to be five attractive high school girls — revealed at the end to be five middle-aged adult men who had been made up by Shiseido. The twist demonstrated makeup's transformative power with absolute precision.

## Why it worked

Absurdism meets design as idea: the reveal was not just a trick — it was the product demonstration. The reaction of audiences confronting their assumptions about gender and beauty did Shiseido's brief better than any claim. The film spread globally because the reveal demanded to be shared.

## Steal

If your product transforms something, find the most extreme possible demonstration of that transformation. The audience's gasp of recognition is the brief being completed. The more their assumptions are implicated, the more they share.
