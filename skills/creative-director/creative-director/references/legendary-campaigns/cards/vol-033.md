# This is SportsCenter (ESPN · 1995)

## Insight
Sports fans love athletes but also mock them tenderly — they know them well enough to laugh at them. ESPN's anchor hosts have credibility with fans but are rarely celebrated. The ESPN headquarters in Bristol, Connecticut is a comedy setting hiding in plain sight: what if the athletes really worked there?

## Mechanic
A long-running campaign of 30-second spots set in the ESPN Bristol headquarters, treating it as a mundane office where athletes, coaches, and anchors coexist in absurd situations — a basketball player helping with the copy machine, a hockey player in the break room. Deadpan delivery; no winking.

## Why it worked
The campaign has run for nearly three decades because the format is inexhaustible — any athlete, any sport, any mundane office scenario. The joke is structural, not situational, which is why it doesn't age. The casting of athletes in ordinary contexts validates ESPN's authentic relationship with sports while differentiating it from the reverent, drama-first approach of competitors.

## Steal
When you have a long-term campaign, build it around a format rather than a message. A format can accommodate any cultural moment, any new talent, any shift in sports. A message becomes outdated; a format becomes a ritual.

# Bloodnormal (Essity / Libresse · 2017)

## Insight
For fifty years, every feminine hygiene advertisement in the world had used blue liquid to demonstrate absorbency. Real menstrual blood — red — was a taboo so entrenched nobody questioned it. Libresse decided to show blood that was actually blood.

## Mechanic
A film and integrated campaign showing menstrual blood as red, not blue, across multiple life situations — a woman in a pool, sheets, everyday moments. The campaign ran across TV and OOH in multiple markets. Simple, honest, quietly revolutionary.

## Why it worked
The simplest possible behaviour inversion (P03): change the colour of one liquid. Because the convention had held for fifty years, the reversal was seismic — generating global press coverage, industry-wide conversation, and, eventually, category-wide change. Truth-telling (P13): stating the obvious that the entire industry had colluded to avoid.

## Steal
Find the most arbitrary rule in your category — the one everyone follows without questioning. Breaking it directly and literally, without irony or drama, is often more powerful than any creative concept. The longer the rule has held, the louder the disruption.

# Periodsomnia (Essity / Libresse · 2022)

## Insight
73% of women report that menstrual pain disrupts their sleep — one of the most widely shared physical experiences in the world, never shown in advertising. The category had been talking about periods in daylight for decades while the night-time reality remained invisible.

## Mechanic
A surrealist film depicting women's menstrual suffering at night — visualised through transformation, monsters, crawling, nightmare sequences. No euphemism, no blue liquid, no smiling woman in white trousers. The product appears as relief, not the hero.

## Why it worked
Libresse's consistent escalation strategy (Bloodnormal → Viva la Vulva → Womb Stories → Periodsomnia) means each new execution must break a new taboo. Having established that the brand shows the truth, audiences expect — and want — each iteration to go further. The behaviour inversion (P03) is from "sanitised category imagery" to "surrealist horror"; the truth-telling (P13) is naming a universal experience that the category had collectively avoided for a generation.

## Steal
Once a brand has broken one category taboo, the audience expects escalation. Retreating to convention after a breakthrough is the single most damaging creative decision a challenger brand can make.

# Viva la Vulva (Essity / Libresse · 2018)

## Insight
Women have no visual reference for what a "normal" vulva looks like — the only representation in mainstream culture is pornographic, creating widespread body anxiety about something every woman has. The anatomy had been systematically excluded from visual culture.

## Mechanic
A musical film and integrated campaign featuring vulvas represented through everyday objects — shells, fruits, flowers, sculptures — all singing a celebratory song. Joyful, aesthetically rich, anatomically specific without being clinical. OOH and social extensions.

## Why it worked
Behaviour inversion (P03): fashion and beauty advertising had eliminated the vulva from representation; Libresse built an entire campaign around it. Design as idea (P17): the objects chosen as visual metaphors were carefully selected to be beautiful, not shocking — making the campaign accessible to the broadest possible audience. The humour was the Trojan horse that delivered a message that drama would have made defensive.

## Steal
Serious body-image or health topics are often better approached through aesthetic delight and gentle humour than through earnest drama. Beauty lowers defences; seriousness raises them. The audience leans in when the thing is beautiful.

# Womb Stories (Essity / Libresse · 2020)

## Insight
A woman's reproductive life is not a single narrative — it encompasses menstruation, endometriosis, miscarriage, fertility treatment, pregnancy, birth, menopause, and everything in between. The category had shown only selected moments from this journey, systematically excluding the difficult ones.

## Mechanic
An extended film combining animation and live action to portray the full spectrum of women's physical and emotional experiences across their reproductive lives — from first period to menopause, including miscarriage, failed fertility treatment, and the chronic pain of endometriosis. Animation was used specifically because it could render internal, invisible states that cameras cannot reach.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16) was essential — only an extended format could hold the emotional range across so many different experiences without feeling reductive. Truth-telling (P13): Libresse's platform escalation reached its highest point here — not just breaking taboos but actively filling in the stories the whole category had left blank. The animation technique was not decorative but structurally necessary.

## Steal
When the experience you need to depict is internal, emotional, or physically invisible, animation is not a stylistic choice but a technical solution. Use it where live action creates a biological impossibility — the interior of the body, the texture of pain.
