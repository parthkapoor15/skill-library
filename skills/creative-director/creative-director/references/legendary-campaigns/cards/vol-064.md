# Hold Music (Little Caesars · 2019)

## Insight
Being put on hold when ordering food is a small but real indignity. Little Caesars' Hot-N-Ready model eliminates waiting entirely — the frustration of the alternative is the clearest argument for the product.

## Mechanic
A TV spot dramatizing the absurdity of being kept on hold while trying to order pizza elsewhere, with Little Caesars' Hot-N-Ready positioned as the obvious escape. The hold music becomes the comedic antagonist.

## Why it worked
The universal micro-annoyance of hold music is exaggerated to absurdist lengths, turning a minor friction point into a relatable problem with a clear solution. The simplicity of the Hot-N-Ready model doesn't need explanation when the alternative is this ridiculous.

## Steal
Find the smallest, most universal friction in the category that your product eliminates. Amplify that friction to absurdity. The bigger the gap between the problem and the solution, the more persuasive the product benefit feels.

# Log In (Little Caesars · 2019)

## Insight
Pizza delivery apps have proliferated to the point of confusion. Little Caesars, with its Hot-N-Ready model and own ordering app, sits in a different category — no wait, no complexity. The app is the shortcut most people haven't tried yet.

## Mechanic
An absurdist TV spot promoting Little Caesars' Pizza Portal pickup app, dramatizing the chaos of alternative ordering methods versus the simplicity of logging in and picking up.

## Why it worked
The spot exaggerates the friction of not using the app through absurdist dramatization, making the product benefit (simplicity) feel earned rather than stated. Part of a campaign ecosystem reframing Little Caesars as the smart, effortless choice.

## Steal
When a technology product exists to remove friction, don't demonstrate the product — demonstrate the friction it removes, pushed to absurd extremes. The gap between the absurd alternative and the calm solution is the message.

# Field Trip to Mars (Lockheed Martin · 2016)

## Insight
Lockheed Martin is building the technology to take humans to Mars — but the people who most need to understand and support that mission are not the current generation of voters and taxpayers; they are the children who will live in that world. The most effective case for a Mars mission is making children feel they are already going.

## Mechanic
A school bus fitted with augmented reality — transparent screens replacing the windows — that transformed a normal school field trip into a simulated journey to Mars. As the bus drove through Washington DC, the windows showed a photorealistic Martian landscape synced to the bus's actual movement. Children experienced Mars as a real place without leaving the city.

## Why it worked
P18 (tech as canvas): the technology is not a demonstration but the experience itself. P09 (user as co-author): children became participants in a journey rather than audience for an advertisement. The Cannes Grand Prix validated that B2B brands can earn attention through human experiences that have nothing to do with product features. Lockheed Martin did not explain its Mars mission; it made children feel it.

## Steal
The most credible argument for a future technology is making people experience it in the present. If your brand is building something that doesn't exist yet, find the closest possible present-tense simulation of that future and let people live in it briefly.

# Ivan Cobenk (Logitech · 2011)

## Insight
Technology advertising usually features the product heroically solving serious problems. The absurdist alternative: what if the tech product attracted attention via the most mundane, bizarre human behaviour imaginable?

## Mechanic
A film following the fictional Ivan Cobenk — a man doing something inexplicably strange with or around a Logitech product. The absurdism serves as attention device; the product is present throughout.

## Why it worked
Absurdist content travels in an attention economy because it cannot be predicted or summarised. The product presence without product lecture gave Logitech a distinctive tone in a category dominated by feature demonstrations.

## Steal
Tech products in undifferentiated categories can escape feature-comparison advertising through tonal differentiation. The product doesn't need to be the hero of every moment — it needs to be consistently present in an ownable tonal universe.

# 100 Years (Louis XIII · 2015)

## Insight
Louis XIII cognac takes 100 years to make. Any brand claim about patience or craft is undermined by conventional advertising's immediacy. The only honest way to communicate a century-long product is to create communication that cannot be consumed for a century.

## Mechanic
John Malkovich starred in a film about 2115 that was locked in a fireproof safe and will only be screened on November 18, 2115. Only 1,000 tickets were issued — to people who will almost certainly be dead when the film premieres. The trailer was released publicly.

## Why it worked
The mechanic IS the message. A brand that will not reveal its film for 100 years demonstrates, rather than claims, that it thinks in century-long terms. The inaccessibility creates desire. The absurdist elegance generates global press coverage for a product that almost no one can afford.

## Steal
Find the one structural commitment that cannot be faked. Not a claim — a lock, a contract, a physical restriction. The constraint becomes the communication.
