# Mr. W (Epuron · 2006)

## Insight
Wind energy was abstract and invisible as a concept. Epuron, a German wind energy company, needed to make wind tangible and sympathetic. The insight: wind is everywhere, it affects everyone, but it is always the invisible disruptor — it messes hair, blows papers, knocks things over. What if wind had feelings about this?

## Mechanic
A 3-minute short film following a melancholic man (Mr. Wind) who tries to fit into human society but keeps accidentally causing chaos — blowing over displays, scattering papers, disrupting conversations. He falls in love. He cannot stop his nature. In the final scene he generates electricity for a lamp — for the first time, his power becomes useful. He finds his purpose.

## Why it worked
P11 absurdism: personifying wind as a depressed social outcast is surreal but internally logical — the film commits completely. P16 long-form drama: the 3-minute format allowed genuine character development and emotional investment in a non-human protagonist. The emotional payoff (wind finds purpose as energy) translated directly to the brand promise without any claim being made. Cannes-winning work for a company no consumer had ever heard of — the film spread because it was genuinely moving.

## Steal
Personify the invisible or the abstract. Give wind a face, give software a voice, give data a body. The personification must be internally consistent and emotionally true — the audience must care about the character before they care about the product.

# Born Into It (ESPN · 2012)

## Insight
Sports fandom is not chosen — it is inherited. Team loyalty is passed down through family, neighborhood, and generation. The irrational depth of sports devotion makes sense only when understood as identity transmitted through love, not logic.

## Mechanic
A long-form film documenting the way sports fandom is transmitted intergenerationally — a child who is a fan not because they chose to be, but because a parent or grandparent made them one. The film treats the inheritance of team loyalty as a form of cultural legacy.

## Why it worked
ESPN's role is to serve fans — and the deepest fans are those for whom fandom is identity, not hobby. By honoring the inherited nature of that identity, the film positions ESPN not as entertainment but as the keeper of something genuinely meaningful in people's lives. The emotion is bittersweet: the loved one who passed on the fandom may no longer be there.

## Steal
When your audience's relationship with a category is not rational but inherited and identity-level, honor the source of that inheritance rather than the category itself. The family member who created the fan is the insight, not the sport.

# It's Not Crazy, It's Sports (ESPN · 2002)

## Insight
Sports fans behave in ways that would be certifiably insane in any other context — crying over strangers, screaming at screens, wearing grown men's names on their backs. The insight is that this is not embarrassing; it is the only honest form of adult tribalism left.

## Mechanic
A long-running campaign of absurdist vignettes, each showing a fan or athlete doing something inexplicably extreme, ending with the line "It's Not Crazy, It's Sports." The spots do not explain ESPN — they validate the audience's existing emotional state and label it as belonging.

## Why it worked
P11 (absurd as carrier) reframes fan obsession not as embarrassing but as the point. P16 (long-form drama within each spot) builds small emotional arcs — the escalation from mundane to insane. The tagline does not describe a product; it pardons a behavior. ESPN becomes the brand that understands sports fans more than sports fans understand themselves.

## Steal
Find the behavior your audience does that they feel they should apologize for. Instead of softening it, name it and pardon it. The brand that gives people permission to be exactly who they already are builds deeper loyalty than any product promise.

# This is SportsCenter — Best-of Compilation (ESPN · 1995–ongoing)

## Insight
The "This is SportsCenter" campaign's longevity means it functions as a cultural archive of sports history. The best-of list is itself a media artifact — evidence that a campaign became a canon.

## Mechanic
A curated compilation of the campaign's most memorable spots, surfaced through editorial lists and fan recommendations. Individual spots within the series range from legendary (LeBron, Manning) to obscure, but the aggregate forms a portrait of American sports culture across three decades.

## Why it worked
Compilations extend campaign life through nostalgia and discovery. Each list surfaces the campaign to non-traditional audiences and gives current fans access to spots that predate their fandom. The format itself becomes another distribution mechanism.

## Steal
Build campaigns designed to be anthologized. Each execution should be independently shareable and strong enough to stand without the series. The series then aggregates into a cultural record.
