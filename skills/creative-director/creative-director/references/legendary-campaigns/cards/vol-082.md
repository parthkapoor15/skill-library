# The Man Your Man Could Smell Like (Old Spice · 2010)

## Insight
Old Spice was your grandfather's deodorant — irrelevant, dated, associated with age rather than aspiration. Women buy 60% of men's grooming products for the men in their lives. The real audience was the woman who wanted her partner to be better, and the real promise was not smell but transformation fantasy.

## Mechanic
A single-take 30-second spot featuring Isaiah Mustafa escalating through increasingly impossible environments (bathroom → boat → horse) while delivering a stream of hyperbolic claims in a direct address to "the ladies," demonstrating the impossible life upgrade that Old Spice body wash apparently provides. Followed by an interactive campaign where Mustafa responded to celebrity and fan tweets with personalized videos in real time.

## Why it worked
Three simultaneous inversions: (1) addressed women directly in a men's grooming ad; (2) deployed absurdist escalation instead of masculine gravitas; (3) acknowledged the absurdity while fully committing to it — ironic sincerity at maximum. The product became the punchline of a joke that made you want the punchline. The real-time response campaign extended the platform into a genuine media event.

## Steal
When the category takes itself seriously, absurd escalation played completely straight is the sharpest available weapon. The joke must be structural, not situational — the absurdity must live in the format, not in a gag, so it can sustain an entire campaign.

# Mom Song (Old Spice · 2012)

## Insight
Mothers buy grooming products for their sons, then sons grow up and a girlfriend or wife buys them differently. The transition from "boy who smells like my son" to "man who smells like someone else's man" is a genuine and underexplored maternal grief.

## Mechanic
A Super Bowl film in which mothers sing a lament as their sons leave home and begin dating — the grooming product transition dramatised as a comedic operatic tragedy. Absurd production values (theatrical staging, weeping mothers, operatic delivery) are played completely straight. Part of the "Smellcome to Manhood" platform.

## Why it worked
Absurd as carrier (P11): the operatic staging applied to the mundane reality of a grooming product purchase creates a comedic gap that is both funny and genuinely tender. Long-form drama (P16): the extended musical format is only possible with premium production and timing; it earns its length through the joke's escalation. The emotion (maternal separation anxiety) is real even when the execution is absurd.

## Steal
Apply a maximalist artistic form (opera, ballet, Greek tragedy) to a minimal consumer truth and play it completely straight. The contrast between form and subject is the comedy. The underlying emotion must be genuine or the joke collapses.

# WoMen's Football (Orange · 2024)

## Insight
Women's football is systematically undervalued — by audiences, media, and even fans who claim to support it. The film exposes this bias by exploiting it and then reversing it.

## Mechanic
A film that appears to show spectacular male football skills from the French national team. At the reveal, it is shown that the skills belong to the women's team — the men's footage was VFX overlay. The tagline: "Support the women's team as you support the men's." Won Cannes Grand Prix.

## Why it worked
Truth telling (P13): the reveal is not a twist for entertainment — it is proof of the audience's own bias. You watched the skills and assumed they belonged to men. Behavior inversion (P03): the film inverts the expectation and then names what you just did. The viewer is implicated; the message is undeniable.

## Steal
The most powerful argument for equality is the one the audience makes against themselves. Construct a situation where the audience's own assumption becomes the data. The reveal does not need to be angry — it just needs to be irrefutable.

# Dunk in the Dark (Oreo · 2013)

## Insight
Real-time cultural relevance is structurally impossible for most brands because approvals, legal, and production timelines are incompatible with the speed of live events. The insight: if you build the infrastructure and permissions structure before the event, you can move as fast as the internet.

## Mechanic
During Super Bowl XLVII's 34-minute power outage on February 3, 2013, Oreo's social team posted a single image to Twitter: a partially lit Oreo cookie with the copy "You can still dunk in the dark." The tweet went up within minutes of the blackout starting. By morning it had 15,000 retweets and international media coverage. Total cost: essentially zero.

## Why it worked
P05 (cultural hijack): the blackout was the cultural moment; Oreo borrowed the attention of 108 million people watching the Super Bowl, adding something rather than merely referencing. P09 (user as co-author): the tweet was designed to be retweeted — its concision, wit, and relevance gave people a reason to share it as their own cultural observation. The real innovation was organizational: a pre-approved real-time team with the authority to publish without a 48-hour approval chain.

## Steal
Build the capability before you need it. A war room is not a creative strategy — it is an organizational strategy. The creative idea (dunking in the dark) is simple. The hard work was convincing an organization to trust a small team to publish live, unsupervised, during the most watched television event of the year.

# OreoCodes (Oreo · 2024)

## Insight
Every Oreo cookie has a unique embossed pattern — a physical detail that billions of people have touched but never interpreted as a code.

## Mechanic
Oreo turned the cookie's surface pattern into scannable codes that unlocked rewards, content, and collectible experiences, making the physical product an interactive digital interface.

## Why it worked
The product itself became the medium. Users had an existing object that suddenly had new utility — zero barrier to entry, since everyone already has an Oreo. The mechanic rewarded attention to something previously unnoticed.

## Steal
Find the overlooked physical detail of your product and make it load-bearing. Existing artifacts become participation mechanics when you give them new interpretive weight.
