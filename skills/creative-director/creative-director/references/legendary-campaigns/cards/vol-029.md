# Un Rendez Vous (Dior Homme · 2008)

## Insight
Luxury masculine fragrance advertising is trapped between machismo (sport, action, conquest) and sensitive aesthetics (art, nature, melancholy). Dior Homme found a third territory: the cinematic love affair — romantic, French, slightly dangerous.

## Mechanic
A short film directed with cinematic craft (P08) in the vein of French nouvelle vague — a fleeting encounter, a rendezvous, ambiguity about what happens next. Minimal product presence (P07). Long-form narrative (P16) creating a mood rather than telling a story.

## Why it worked
Luxury fragrance campaigns that achieve cultural longevity are those that function as short films rather than ads. P08 (craft as message) is essential: the quality of direction signals the quality of the fragrance without any product claim. P07 (invisible brand) works because Dior Homme's codes are so established that the brand can absent itself from the frame and remain present through tone.

## Steal
Luxury products that are experienced through the senses (fragrance, taste) cannot be demonstrated — they can only be evoked. The craft of the evocation is the product proof.

# Get Rid of Cable (DirecTV · 2014)

## Insight
Cable subscriptions are a source of daily frustration — outages, bundles of channels nobody wants, service calls that don't show up. But people stay because switching feels like effort. The insight: dramatize the downstream consequences of keeping cable to a ridiculous logical extreme.

## Mechanic
A series of spots following the chain-reaction logic of "when you have cable, you get angry, and when you get angry, you do X" — escalating through absurdist consequences until the viewer ends up in some spectacular disaster. Each spot ends with DirecTV's proposition. The campaign starred Rob Lowe playing both himself (the DirecTV user, confident and successful) and an alternate-universe version of himself degraded by cable dependency.

## Why it worked
P11 (absurd as carrier): the chain-reaction narrative is a classic comedy structure, but applied with enough commitment and escalation to feel genuinely cinematic rather than merely clever. P16: Rob Lowe playing opposite himself requires performance calibration across two registers simultaneously — the joke works because both versions are played with equal seriousness. The spots were genuinely rewatched and shared.

## Steal
The "logic chain" comedy structure (when X happens, Y follows, which leads to Z...) works when each step is surprising while remaining plausible within the universe of the ad. The comedy collapses when any step is too obvious or too arbitrary.

# Our Blades Are F***ing Great (Dollar Shave Club · 2012)

## Insight
The shaving category was dominated by Gillette's over-engineered, over-priced hardware. Consumers knew they were being gouged but had no credible alternative. A DTC startup with no media budget needed to detonate the category with a single shot.

## Mechanic
A $4,500 walk-and-talk film: CEO Michael Dubin strides through the warehouse making absurdist jokes, mocking overpriced blades, and promising blades that are "fucking great" for $1/month. No production value. No agency. Pure founder voice. 12,000 orders in the first 48 hours. 27M YouTube views in 60 days.

## Why it worked
Absurd as carrier (P11): comedy disarmed skepticism in a category built on pseudo-science claims. Long-form drama (P16): the two-minute run time was justified by laugh density — every sentence earned its stay. The founder's face as brand asset is irreplaceable; it made every claim feel like a personal warranty. The profanity was functional: it signaled "we will tell you the truth."

## Steal
Founder-led video with genuine humor is the cheapest and most defensible DTC launch vehicle. Low production value signals authenticity only if the writing is sharp enough to carry it. Profanity works as a credibility marker, not a shock tactic, when the product claim behind it is real.

# World's Biggest Asshole (Donate Life America · 2016)

## Insight
Organ donor registration feels morally heavy and bureaucratically dull — the message attracts only the already-converted. But even the worst person can save four lives.

## Mechanic
A film tracking the most objectionable man imaginable — he lies, steals, insults everyone — who dies in a brawl. Final card: "Coleman donated his organs and saved 4 lives. He may have been the world's biggest asshole — and the world's biggest hero."

## Why it worked
The moral inversion completely disarms the "I'm not good enough to be a donor" barrier. Registration is democratized: even a terrible person qualifies. Absurdism lowers defenses so the message lands without preachiness.

## Steal
Remove the moral bar from a behaviour-change ask. Show that even the least qualified person can do it — this inverts the usual "good people do X" frame and makes the action feel accessible to everyone.

# DoorDash-All-the-Ads (DoorDash · 2024)

## Insight
Super Bowl ads spend millions to offer viewers fantasy. DoorDash offered to actually deliver the products from every advertiser's spot — turning the cultural ritual of watching Super Bowl ads into a shopping event with a single promo code.

## Mechanic
DoorDash ran one Super Bowl spot that offered a promo code containing every product advertised during the game — from M&Ms to Budweiser to Nerds — in one giant delivery order. The promo code itself ("DOOORDASH" with extra Os, hidden in a rapid-fire visual sequence) was a puzzle audiences had to solve in real time. The campaign ran as an integrated digital + broadcast event.

## Why it worked
P05 (cultural hijack) of the Super Bowl advertising ecosystem — DoorDash turned every other brand's expensive media buy into free creative material for its own campaign. P11 (absurd as carrier) via the impossibly large delivery order as comedy. P02 (enemy or conflict) redefined competitors as complements — the more valuable the other brands' ads, the more valuable the DoorDash offer. The promo code mechanic created active participation during a passive viewing event.

## Steal
When your category is full of competitors, make their marketing budgets work for you. If your delivery or distribution covers what others advertise, you can turn their spend into your proof of relevance.
