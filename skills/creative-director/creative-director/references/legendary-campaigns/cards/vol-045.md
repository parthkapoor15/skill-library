# Try to Contain Your Excitement (Harvey Nichols · 2009)

## Insight
British understatement applied to luxury shopping — the politely suppressed excitement of the Harvey Nichols customer is more distinctive than overt enthusiasm.

## Mechanic
Outdoor posters using the driest possible copywriting to describe items that would typically warrant excitement — communicating the brand's ironic, self-aware British tone through deliberate anti-climax.

## Why it worked
The tonal gap between the quality of the product and the deadpan copy signals cultural sophistication. Understatement is a class marker; the customer who gets it feels identified as "the right type."

## Steal
Express maximum product quality through minimum emotional register. The understatement signals that the audience is too sophisticated to need enthusiasm explained to them.

# For the Throne (HBO · 2019)

## Insight
Game of Thrones had created a global community unified by a single narrative tension: who would take the Iron Throne? Before the final season, that anticipation was the most valuable cultural moment HBO had ever owned. The campaign's job was not to build awareness — it was to channel existing obsession.

## Mechanic
An integrated campaign that invited fans to pledge allegiance to their chosen claimant — Daenerys, Jon, Cersei, etc. — through physical experiences, branded collaborations (Bud Light, Mountain Dew, Oreo), social content, and real-world events. The "For the Throne" framing made every consumer action a declaration of loyalty.

## Why it worked
User as co-author (P09): every pledge, share, and debate made fans active participants in the pre-season narrative. Serialization and ritual (P10): the show had built multi-year rituals (watch parties, recap culture, theory communities) that the campaign extended into physical form. The collaborations were genuine — Bud Light made a GoT Super Bowl spot; Oreo recreated the opening sequence in cookies.

## Steal
When you have a property with a built-in, fanatical audience, the campaign is to give that audience something to do — not something to watch. Convert passive viewership into active allegiance through real-world mechanics that cost nothing to participate in.

# Acceptance (HBO Go · 2014)

## Insight
When a family member who disapproves of your lifestyle choices watches HBO content with you that validates or depicts that lifestyle, the shared viewing becomes an involuntary disclosure and confrontation.

## Mechanic
A short film in the "Awkward Family Viewing" series exploring the layered discomfort when HBO content inadvertently surfaces personal truths in a family viewing situation — with HBO Go offered as the exit from communal screening.

## Why it worked
The series works as a campaign because each film targets a specific relationship configuration and discomfort type, building a taxonomy of awkward viewing that collectively covers a wide audience while each individual film feels precisely targeted.

## Steal
Design a campaign series where each execution isolates a single, specific instance of a broader insight. The series covers the full insight; each film earns its place by naming something the others don't.

# Appreciation (HBO Go · 2014)

## Insight
Sharing a genuinely appreciated HBO show with someone who doesn't connect with it in the same way creates a specific loneliness — you wanted to share something meaningful and it failed.

## Mechanic
A short film in the "Awkward Family Viewing" series depicting the discomfort of watching something you love with someone who doesn't appreciate it — the reverse of standard awkward viewing, where the content itself is uncomfortable. HBO Go becomes the solution: watch alone and keep the experience intact.

## Why it worked
The series closure adds a final dimension: it's not just the content that's awkward — the mismatch between viewer and co-viewer about what's valuable is itself uncomfortable. The campaign covers both directions of the problem.

## Steal
In a campaign series, include the inverse of the core scenario. The same product can solve the same problem from opposite directions — both "I don't want to watch this with them" and "they don't want to watch this with me."

# Awkward Family Viewing (HBO Go · 2014)

## Insight
HBO's explicit content creates genuinely awkward viewing situations when parents, children or coworkers are in the room — a shared cultural embarrassment no one discusses.

## Mechanic
A series of comedy spots dramatizing the specific agony of watching HBO content with the wrong companion: nudity scenes with parents, violence with in-laws, sex scenes with coworkers. Each scenario ends with a pitch for HBO Go — watch on your own device, alone.

## Why it worked
The campaign named a real behavior and turned product utility (personal mobile viewing) into a social escape hatch. Absurdist comedy made the awkwardness shareable rather than shameful.

## Steal
Find the most embarrassing use-case for your product and make it the hero scenario. Naming the awkward truth creates recognition and positions the solution simultaneously.
