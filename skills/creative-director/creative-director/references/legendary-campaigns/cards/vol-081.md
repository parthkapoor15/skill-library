# Freeze (New Zealand Police · 2017)

## Insight
Police recruitment advertising is invariably earnest, authoritative, and forgettable. The people least likely to apply are exactly those who distrust institutional seriousness. What if a police recruitment campaign behaved like the opposite of a police recruitment campaign?

## Mechanic
New Zealand Police released a recruitment video where officers perform an elaborate Bollywood-style song and dance sequence, freezing mid-action at intervals to deliver recruitment messages. The video went globally viral and dramatically increased applications.

## Why it worked
The absurdist tone (P11) signals that the NZ Police are not the sterile institution recruits fear. The self-awareness of the joke communicates confidence. People who share the video because it's funny are simultaneously spreading the recruitment message to exactly the demographic the force wants.

## Steal
In institutional categories where earnestness is the default, absurdism signals confidence and honesty. The willingness to look silly means the institution isn't precious about itself — which is exactly the cultural signal that recruits value.

# Smoooth (Oatly · 2018)

## Insight
Oatly's CEO Tony Petersson playing piano and singing about oat milk in a Swedish field is arguably the brand's most unguarded creative act. The deliberate awkwardness is the brand's most compelling product demonstration: a company this unafraid of looking ridiculous cannot be hiding anything.

## Mechanic
A film of Oatly CEO singing a soft-rock ballad about "wow no cow" — deliberately amateurish, deeply earnest, and structurally anti-advertising. The song ("Wow, No Cow!") became a minor internet cultural artefact.

## Why it worked
P08 (craft as message) inverted — the complete absence of craft is the craft. P16 gives the performance enough time to become genuinely strange. No other food brand could run this film without looking confused; Oatly's established anti-corporate voice makes the awkwardness read as courage rather than failure.

## Steal
The most credible proof of values is being willing to look foolish in their defence. If a brand's anti-corporate stance is real, it should be able to survive — and benefit from — an execution that no conventionally-run brand would approve.

# Where There Are Cooks (Oatly · 2019)

## Insight
Oatly's brand voice is one of the most distinctive in food — irreverent, anti-corporate, and self-aware. The challenge is extending that voice into long-form without losing the spontaneity that makes it feel genuine.

## Mechanic
A film following cooks and chefs who use Oatly products — presented with Oatly's characteristic anti-advertising aesthetic: imperfect production, honest narration, and the brand's habitual refusal to follow food marketing conventions.

## Why it worked
P08 (craft as message) in an unusual form — the deliberate lo-fi aesthetic of Oatly's production quality is itself the brand proof. A brand that refuses to look like advertising, even in a film, demonstrates that its anti-corporate stance is genuine rather than strategic. P16 gives it enough time to build a world.

## Steal
If your brand's position is anti-advertising, the worst thing you can do is make a polished ad. Commit to the aesthetic that your stance demands — the production quality is the argument.

# Dad Song (Old Spice · 2014)

## Insight
The companion to Mom Song. Fathers experience the same loss as their sons grow up and transition to adult grooming — but fathers express it differently, less vocally, with the same underlying emotion under a more stoic surface.

## Mechanic
A follow-up film to Mom Song, playing the same operatic absurdist conceit from the father's perspective. The tonal shift — from maternal expressiveness to paternal restraint, both played for the same comedic effect — extends the platform into new emotional territory.

## Why it worked
Absurd as carrier (P11): the formula is consistent — operatic melodrama applied to the mundane product moment — which makes the tonal variation (dad vs. mom) the new joke rather than requiring a new concept. Long-form drama (P16): the musical structure requires and justifies extended runtime. Platform thinking: the same mechanic generates multiple executions without repeating itself.

## Steal
A strong platform idea can be extended by varying one variable at a time. Mom → Dad is a simple variation that doubles your content while remaining recognizable. Don't change the concept; change the character.

# Legendary Man (Old Spice · 2015)

## Insight
Old Spice's absurdist masculine mythology — established by "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like" — was a brand platform flexible enough to generate new characters and escalations indefinitely. Each new execution extends the mythology rather than restating the original.

## Mechanic
A new iteration in Old Spice's absurdist masculine mythology — introducing or extending a character archetype of hyperbolically legendary masculinity, delivered in the brand's signature deadpan-absurdist register with production values that support the comedic escalation.

## Why it worked
Absurd as carrier (P11): the established brand tone allows each execution to pick up where the last left off without re-establishing the joke. Long-form drama (P16): the character study format rewards investment — the longer the film runs, the deeper the absurdism goes. Brand platform thinking at its most functional: the same creative logic generates infinite executions.

## Steal
Build a tonal platform broad enough to generate new characters rather than new concepts. A sufficiently defined tone of voice means the concept writes itself — you only need new characters, contexts, or escalations.
