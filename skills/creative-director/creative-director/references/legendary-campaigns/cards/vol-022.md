# It's Halftime in America (Chrysler · 2012)

## Insight
America in 2012 was mid-recovery from economic crisis — genuinely uncertain whether the comeback was real. The Super Bowl halftime is the moment of suspension between what happened and what comes next. Both the game and the country were at the same inflection point.

## Mechanic
A two-minute Super Bowl spot narrated by Clint Eastwood — a figure who embodies American stoicism — using the game's halftime structure as a direct metaphor for the national economic moment. "The people of Detroit know something about this that the rest of America can learn from." Car imagery minimal; America-as-comeback-story central.

## Why it worked
Eastwood's voice and persona do what no advertising copy can: convey earned credibility. The halftime metaphor is simple and universally legible. The political controversy it generated (accusations of partisan messaging) was itself proof of cultural relevance. The spot was interpreted as both advertising and editorial — which is the highest form of cultural insertion.

## Steal
Find the structural metaphor that maps your brand's situation onto a universal human experience. Not an analogy to be explained, but a metaphor that lands before the copy does. The metaphor should be true, not clever.

# Imported From Detroit (Chrysler · 2011)

## Insight
Detroit had been the subject of bankruptcy coverage, decay narratives, and national pity for years. Chrysler — itself rescued by government bailout — was positioned as a symbol of failure. No one expected a Chrysler car to be excellent. And "imported" was what Americans called cars that were actually good.

## Mechanic
A two-minute Super Bowl spot narrated by Eminem and set entirely in Detroit — its architecture, its people, its music — arguing that the city (and the car) have earned something that cannot be explained to people who haven't been through what they've been through. The tagline: "Imported from Detroit." The car appears for the final ten seconds.

## Why it worked
The ad inverted the grammar of automotive advertising entirely. No driving sequences. No handling claims. No aspirational landscape. Instead: defiant civic pride wrapped in hip-hop cultural credibility (Eminem, born in Detroit, not a celebrity endorser). The P06 logic (limitation as power) inverts the bailout stigma into proof of survival. The brand became the city's voice at a moment when the city had none.

## Steal
When your brand has a scar, make the scar the story. Not because audiences are forgiving, but because authenticity about struggle is the only foundation for credibility with an audience that has also struggled. The scar must be real — manufactured vulnerability reads immediately.

# Chasing Horizons (Citizen · 2018)

## Insight
A watch brand lives at the intersection of time and aspiration. The horizon is the metaphor that connects both: something always ahead, always pursued, never quite reached — which is the emotional territory of both time and ambition.

## Mechanic
A film following individuals across landscapes — physical and metaphorical — as they pursue their version of the horizon. Cinematic and aspirational, with Citizen's light-powered Eco-Drive technology as the enabling product truth (powered by light, like the horizon itself).

## Why it worked
P16 (long-form drama) in the luxury watch tradition of building emotional territory rather than demonstrating product features. The horizon metaphor is broad enough to be universal while giving the film a consistent visual logic. The product's actual attribute — light-powered — provides a grounding link to the metaphor that prevents the campaign from feeling purely decorative.

## Steal
When your product has a functional attribute that can be poetically reframed as a metaphor, let the metaphor carry the film and use the attribute as the anchor. The metaphor earns emotion; the attribute earns credibility.

# Revenge (Clash of Clans · 2015)

## Insight
Mobile gaming has a stigma — "it's just a game." But anyone who's been raided in Clash of Clans knows the sting of real competitive loss. The emotional investment is genuine; the audience just won't admit it openly.

## Mechanic
A Super Bowl ad featuring Liam Neeson as a furious player recording a voice memo to a stranger who destroyed his village in-game — channeling his "Taken" persona into a mobile gaming revenge fantasy. Played completely straight for maximum comedic impact.

## Why it worked
The joke works because the absurdity is grounded in real emotion. Liam Neeson's cinematic intensity applied to a mobile game validated the feeling every player has after a raid without explaining it. The Taken parody made it shareable beyond the gaming audience.

## Steal
When the product experience contains a real emotion the audience is embarrassed to admit, cast it with cultural credibility and play it completely straight. The comedy comes from the gap between the gravitas and the context, not from winking at the audience.

# Stairlift (Clash of Clans · 2015)

## Insight
Age is the last demographic mobile gaming was expected to reach. An elderly player contradicts every assumption about who the game is for — and reveals that the compulsion to raid is fundamentally human, not generational.

## Mechanic
A spot featuring an elderly player on a stairlift, deeply absorbed in Clash of Clans, playing with the same intensity as any teenager. The domestic, slow-paced setting contrasts with the warfare on the screen.

## Why it worked
The archetype inversion is maximally unexpected — stairlift as raid vehicle. Extending the "Revenge" campaign logic to the most demographically incongruous player amplifies the brand's implicit claim: this game is for everyone, and everyone gets obsessed.

## Steal
To communicate universal appeal without claiming it, show the most unlikely user in their most unlikely context, behaving exactly like the core user. The contrast does the work the copy would otherwise explain.
