# Snap (Apple · 2020)

## Insight

Listening to music through great audio is a private performance — when fully immersed in sound, everyday actions become choreography.

## Mechanic

Short film set during lockdown: a woman dancing through her apartment to music via AirPods, every domestic action — cooking, cleaning, stretching — transformed into precise, joyful choreography. Released during COVID confinement, making the domestic setting resonate powerfully.

## Why it worked

Craft as message with perfect cultural timing: the lockdown context made the home-bound dance sequence feel like a release valve. The film celebrated private joy as a form of survival — deeply resonant in context.

## Steal

When external circumstances make your product's use context suddenly universal, build a film that acknowledges the context without explaining it. The audience supplies the emotional weight.

# Stroll (Apple · 2017)

## Insight

Music through quality audio changes the experience of simply moving through space — a walk becomes something else entirely when the soundtrack is right.

## Mechanic

Short film following a person on a walk through a city, with Apple audio (AirPods/Apple Music) transforming the mundane journey into something cinematic — the world syncing with the music.

## Why it worked

Part of Apple's craft-first film series: by showing the interior experience of quality audio through exterior storytelling, the films made intangible audio quality feel visible.

## Steal

Take the most ordinary version of your product use case. Render the internal experience as an external spectacle. The contrast between mundane trigger and extraordinary feeling is the idea.

# Sway (Apple · 2016)

## Insight

Dance is the physical expression of music — when sound is perfect, the body responds without thinking. Apple's audio products enable the purest connection between music and movement.

## Mechanic

Film showcasing extraordinary dance performance set to music through Apple devices — high-craft choreography and cinematography where the movement itself is the product demonstration of audio quality.

## Why it worked

Craft as message: the dancer's precision and expressiveness transferred as implied product quality without any spoken claim. Apple's extended film series used craft excellence as the argument.

## Steal

When you cannot directly demonstrate a sensory product benefit, find the human behavior that emerges from it at its most extreme and beautiful. Film that behavior.

# The Greatest (Apple · 2023)

## Insight

People with disabilities are not "despite" who they are — they are exactly who they are. Apple's accessibility features are not special accommodations; they are how Apple designs for everyone.

## Mechanic

Film showing disabled people doing extraordinary things using iPhone accessibility features — the absurdist angle is that what appears superhuman (seeing without eyes, communicating without voice, navigating without touch) is simply Apple's standard functionality in action.

## Why it worked

Absurdism meets long-form drama: by framing standard accessibility features as "the greatest" human capabilities, the film inverted the usual narrative of disability (limitation → accommodation) into one of capability → freedom. No special treatment, no inspiration porn — just competence.

## Steal

Find the user population your product serves that your category systematically ignores. Make their use case the hero, not the edge case. If the feature is genuinely good, it doesn't need to be framed as a gesture of inclusion.

# Think Different (Apple · 1997)

## Insight
Apple in 1997 was 90 days from bankruptcy. Steve Jobs returned to a company that had lost its identity. The insight: Apple's identity was never about technology — it was about the people who use technology to change the world. Align the brand with every revolutionary, artist, and visionary in history, and you claim that territory without a single product claim.

## Mechanic
A 60-second black-and-white film narrated by Richard Dreyfuss (later Jobs himself), showing photographs of 20th century visionaries — Einstein, Gandhi, Chaplin, Martin Luther King Jr., Picasso, Amelia Earhart, Jim Henson, John Lennon — while the narration speaks directly to the "crazy ones, misfits, rebels, troublemakers." The final frame: the Apple logo and "Think Different." Simultaneously launched as a global poster campaign. No product. No features. No price.

## Why it worked
P17 (design as idea): the typographic simplicity of "Think Different" is the creative act — two words that claim a worldview. P16 (long-form drama): the film's emotional arc builds from individual portraits to a collective statement about what kind of person changes the world. P06 (limitation as power): Apple's near-bankruptcy made the "crazy ones" framing authentic — they were genuinely on the verge of extinction. Restored brand confidence internally before external results materialized. Often cited as the campaign that saved Apple.

## Steal
When a brand has lost its way, the recovery campaign should not sell anything. It should restate who the brand is for. "Think Different" does not describe Apple — it describes Apple's customers, and invites them to see themselves in that description.
