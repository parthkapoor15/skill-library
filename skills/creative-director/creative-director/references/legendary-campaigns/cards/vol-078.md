# The Last Game (Nike · 2014)

## Insight
The World Cup triggers a global debate about risk vs. safety in football: scientific, data-optimized play versus the instinct-driven, unpredictable genius of natural footballers. Nike's brand stands with the latter — the improviser, not the algorithm.

## Mechanic
A six-minute animated film, released ahead of the 2014 World Cup, featuring a cast of Nike footballer characters (Ronaldo, Ribery, Rooney, Neymar animated likenesses) facing a future where cloned, risk-free athletes have replaced the originals. The natural players must come out of retirement for one last game to prove that risk is worth it.

## Why it worked
Animation freed the brand from the limits of real-world production while retaining the personalities of actual world stars. The film functions as a manifesto: Nike explicitly sides with flair over safety, instinct over optimization. Six minutes earned because the world was already paying attention to football.

## Steal
For global sporting events, build a narrative that takes a clear position in the debate audiences are already having. Not "we support the event" — but "we have an opinion about what this event means." Position the brand inside the conversation, not around it.

# Unlimited Future (Nike · 2016)

## Insight
The "Unlimited" platform at the Rio Olympics covered multiple dimensions of the human athletic story. "Unlimited Future" focuses on the athlete not yet known — the child training in a gym somewhere who will one day compete at the highest level.

## Mechanic
A film in the Unlimited series that focuses on young athletes and the future potential of the next generation — showing children training in places that connect to the Rio Olympic stages. The narrative arc moves from present effort to imagined future achievement.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16): the time-compression of "this child training now → this athlete winning later" requires extended treatment to feel emotionally earned. Truth telling (P13): the claim is demonstrably true — every Olympic athlete was once an unknown child in a gym. The future tense creates aspiration; the present tense training footage creates credibility.

## Steal
The unknown athlete working in obscurity is a more interesting protagonist than the known champion. The future is more aspirational than the past. Show the before rather than the after.

# Unlimited Stadium (Nike · 2016)

## Insight
Runners in Manila are motivated by competition but lack the infrastructure and community to experience it regularly — running alone is a discipline problem.

## Mechanic
An LED track in the shape of a running shoe, 200 metres long, where runners race against a digital avatar of their own best time. The personal best was visualized as a competitor running alongside them in real time.

## Why it worked
The technology made the runner's own progress visible as a tangible opponent. Self-competition — normally abstract — became embodied and spatial. The product (running shoes, Nike+) and the experience were inseparable.

## Steal
Turn a runner's invisible internal goal (personal best) into a physical competitor that can be defeated. When abstract motivation becomes concrete opposition, the behaviour becomes dramatically more engaging.

# Unlimited You (Nike · 2016)

## Insight
Nike's core position — every body is an athlete — requires a constant inversion of the received wisdom about who can do what. "Unlimited" as a platform inverts the limit: where culture says "you can't," Nike says "you haven't yet."

## Mechanic
A film in the "Unlimited" campaign series for the Rio Olympics, featuring athletes and non-athletes challenging what their bodies are capable of — structured as an escalating sequence of "this person did this thing no one expected." Narrated with Nike's signature defiant-hopeful tone.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16): the accumulation of unexpected achievement stories builds an emotional case that a single hero story cannot. Behavior inversion (P03): the campaign's logic is to invert the limit — every limitation stated becomes a challenge accepted. The Olympic context provides a global stage for the argument.

## Steal
"Unlimited" as a platform works because it is always in dialogue with a specific limit. The campaign only works if the obstacles are named specifically enough that the inversions feel earned. Generic inspiration is weaker than specific defiance.

# Unlimited Youth (Nike · 2016)

## Insight
The third dimension of the Unlimited platform: youth not as a chronological fact but as a quality of engagement — the athlete who refuses to stop regardless of age. "Unlimited" transcends age when it is a mindset rather than a physical condition.

## Mechanic
A film in the Unlimited series focusing on older athletes who continue to compete and train — inverting the cultural assumption that sport belongs to the young. The story of the veteran athlete who refuses the limits of age is a counterpoint to the youth-focused "Unlimited Future."

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16): the emotional power of an older athlete defying age requires a patient buildup; the payoff is earned. Truth telling (P13): the athletes shown are real; their achievements are documented. The platform's flexibility (future/you/youth as three angles on the same idea) demonstrates how a strong platform generates distinct executions without losing coherence.

## Steal
A platform idea strong enough to address youth, maturity, and age simultaneously is a platform worth investing in. Build the platform first, then let different executions find different audience segments without contradiction.
