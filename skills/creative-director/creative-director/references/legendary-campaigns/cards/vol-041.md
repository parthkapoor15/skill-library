# Speed Tests (Google Chrome · 2010)

## Insight
Browser speed was Chrome's primary competitive advantage over Firefox and IE — but "fast browser" is an abstract claim no one can feel. The challenge: make speed tangible, visual, and undeniable without resorting to spec sheets.

## Mechanic
A series of films testing Chrome's speed against physically fast phenomena: a potato gun, sound, and lightning. Each test was engineered as a genuine scientific demonstration, shot with high-speed cameras. The absurdist comparison (P11) made the demonstrations memorable; the product-as-proof mechanic (P14) made them credible. Chrome consistently matched or beat the comparison object.

## Why it worked
Product demonstration at its most inventive: instead of claiming speed, Chrome demonstrated it against referents the audience could visualize. The potato gun and lightning tests were genuinely engineered — this was not CGI. P14 worked because the demonstrations were physically real, not simulated. P11 elevated them from technical videos to culturally shareable content. The result: Chrome's speed advantage became memorable through absurdist physicality rather than spec comparison.

## Steal
When a product attribute is abstract (speed, power, protection), find a physical analogy that makes it visceral. Engineer the demonstration so it cannot be dismissed as trickery — the physicality of the proof is itself the brand message.

# Dear Sophie (Google · 2011)

## Insight
Google's products are used in every important human moment — but the brand had never made that claim through an actual human story. The insight: a father documents his daughter's entire childhood through Gmail drafts, photos, YouTube videos, and Google searches — and never once does Google feel like the subject.

## Mechanic
A two-minute film shows a father creating a Gmail account for his newborn daughter "Sophie" and populating it with milestones: first words, first steps, first day of school — each documented using different Google products. The campaign's P14 mechanic is the Google suite itself: every product shown is used as it actually exists, demonstrating utility through genuine emotional purpose. P16's long-form format allows the emotional accumulation to build across a childhood compressed into two minutes.

## Why it worked
Product demonstration (P14) achieved at human scale: instead of features, you see use cases that matter. The film is not about Google — it's about a father's love expressed through Google. The emotional truth carries the brand, not the other way around. "Dear Sophie" became one of the most-cited examples of product demonstration done through storytelling rather than specification.

## Steal
Show your product being used in the moments that matter most to people's lives. The product becomes the medium for human connection — and the brand inherits the emotional weight of that connection.

# Parisian Love (Google · 2010)

## Insight
Google Search is used in the most intimate and consequential moments of human life — but no one had ever made that visible. The product has no interface beyond a search box, yet through that box, entire lives unfold. A love story told entirely through search queries would be both the most honest demonstration of the product and the most emotionally resonant possible ad.

## Mechanic
A 52-second film shown at the Super Bowl consisting only of a series of Google searches — "study abroad paris france," "cafes near the louvre," "how to impress a french girl," eventually to "how to assemble a crib" — accompanied by a simple piano score. No actors. No voiceover. The search box is the screen; the queries are the narrative. The product demonstration (P14) is the story (P16): inseparable.

## Why it worked
The Google search interface is the one user interface in the world that everyone recognizes. By making the search box the stage, Google turned its most familiar product moment into a cinematic grammar. The story (American falls in love in Paris, marries, has a child) was universally legible. The film required zero explanation — and ran at Super Bowl, the highest-cost media slot, with the lowest-cost execution imaginable. Cannes Grand Prix recognized it as a genuinely new form of brand communication.

## Steal
Ask: does your product have an interface or moment that is universally recognized? If so, that interface can become the camera, the stage, and the character — you don't need actors, locations, or production budget.

# Ask More (Google Pixel · 2019)

## Insight
People ask their phones for facts but rarely for genuine help with the moments that matter most. The Google Assistant — and by extension the Pixel — could be positioned as a partner in life's real questions, not just its trivial ones.

## Mechanic
Film showing people asking Google Assistant genuinely difficult, personal, and emotionally loaded questions — not just weather queries but questions about parenthood, loss, fear, connection. The phone as companion in real life.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16): emotional resonance requires time — each question and its implications take time to land. The positioning of a technology product around human vulnerability rather than technical capability is rare enough to create differentiation. "Ask more" as invitation rather than instruction.

## Steal
Technology brands that position around human need rather than technical specification find more emotional territory. The question is more interesting than the answer — build campaigns around what your audience is genuinely afraid to ask.

# Life By You, Phone by Google (Google Pixel · 2022)

## Insight
Smartphone photography democratised image-making but did not democratise whose stories get told. Google Pixel was built from the ground up to photograph all skin tones equally — a technical fact that could be communicated as a cultural statement.

## Mechanic
Long-form films showing real people's actual lives — in all their visual variety and colour range — captured by Pixel cameras. The "Life By You" framing emphasises that the phone serves the user's story, not a manufactured aspiration. The product proof is the image quality on screen.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16): the film's emotional effect depends on accumulation — multiple real lives, multiple moments — rather than a single persuasive claim. The product feature (skin-tone camera calibration) is expressed through the subject matter rather than through a spec sheet. Authenticity of real people > aspirational casting.

## Steal
When your product has a technical feature that is actually a social feature, show it in the lives it enables rather than in the lab where it was built. The feature disappears; the life remains.
