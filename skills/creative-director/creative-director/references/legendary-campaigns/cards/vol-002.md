# D Rose Jump Store (Adidas · 2012)

## Insight
Derrick Rose was one of the most explosive NBA players alive — his signature shoe was about vertical leap and athleticism. To promote the D Rose shoe, Adidas asked: what if you actually had to jump for it? What if the price was not money but performance?

## Mechanic
A pop-up store placed D Rose signature shoes on a shelf at a height matching Rose's jump — 10 feet. Anyone who could jump and retrieve the shoes got them free. The store was filmed, participants tried and sometimes succeeded. The footage circulated organically. No paid media required for the video.

## Why it worked
Product-as-proof (implicit) through a participatory mechanic (P09) packaged as a cultural hijack (P05) — a pop-up store that generated its own media through the challenge. The stunt was perfectly calibrated: the height was achievable for genuinely athletic people, which meant the content was both aspirational and occasionally triumphant. The Derrick Rose connection gave the mechanic celebrity credibility without requiring Rose to appear.

## Steal
When your product is about performance, design the retail or activation mechanic as a performance test. The product becomes the reward for proving you deserve it. The mechanic becomes the campaign.

# Hello Tomorrow (Adidas · 2018)

## Insight
Adidas operates in a category where heritage and innovation are in perpetual tension. The brand's German engineering legacy meets global youth culture. "Hello Tomorrow" may have positioned Adidas as the brand looking forward rather than back.

## Mechanic
A long-form film (P16) potentially inverting the category convention of nostalgia-led sports brand storytelling (P03), positioning Adidas as future-oriented rather than heritage-focused.

## Why it worked
In a market where Nike owns present-tense athletic achievement and Adidas owns heritage, a campaign that claims future territory creates a new competitive space. P03 (behavior inversion) would work here by abandoning heritage — the strongest card in the deck — in favor of forward momentum.

## Steal
The most powerful positioning move is often to abandon your strongest existing asset and claim the territory everyone else is ignoring. In sportswear, everyone claims innovation — no one had claimed tomorrow.

# Impossible Goalkeeper (Adidas · 2019)

## Insight
Sports equipment advertising claims to help you perform better — but the claims are abstract. An experiential mechanic that lets people test against an "impossible" goalkeeper makes the performance claim physical and participatory.

## Mechanic
An experiential activation (P09) featuring an "impossible goalkeeper" — likely a robotic or algorithmically enhanced opponent — that participants attempt to beat. The absurdist element (P11) is in the "impossible" framing. Adidas equipment or technology helps level the playing field.

## Why it worked
Product-as-proof through participation: people experience the brand's performance claim rather than watching it. The "impossible" framing creates a challenge narrative that generates social sharing through personal achievement.

## Steal
Build the product demonstration into a participatory challenge. The challenge creates stakes; the brand helps you meet them. Experience beats assertion every time.

# Original is Never Finished (Adidas Originals · 2017)

## Insight
The word "original" in cultural discourse doesn't mean "the first" — it means "the source from which everything else is derived." Adidas Originals is not the first sneaker; it is the one that every subsequent sneaker references. The original is never finished because culture keeps reinterpreting it.

## Mechanic
A two-minute film sampling Frank Sinatra's "My Way" with contemporary artists covering it in radically different styles — each version a different cultural reinterpretation of the same source. Images of cultural icons and Adidas Originals athletes intercut with archival and contemporary footage. The film was a deliberate provocation: it recontextualised cultural icons in ways that generated controversy and conversation.

## Why it worked
P16 (long-form drama) as cultural essay. P09 (user as co-author): the controversy around the film's recontextualisation of cultural figures generated massive organic discussion. The "My Way" hook was a formal argument for the brand idea: the song has been covered hundreds of times; each cover is an original act of interpretation. Adidas is the sneaker that culture keeps reinterpreting — which is the definition of an original.

## Steal
When your brand idea is about cultural inheritance, find a cultural artefact that demonstrates that idea structurally, not symbolically. The multiple covers of "My Way" don't illustrate the brand idea — they enact it.

# Until We All Belong (Airbnb · 2017)

## Insight
In Australia, a national debate over marriage equality had reached a postal vote that excluded millions from participating in their own rights. A brand built on the idea that "belonging" is universal had an authentic stake in the outcome.

## Mechanic
Airbnb created an "Acceptance Ring" — a ring made with a deliberate gap in it, symbolising the gap in equality — and distributed it to supporters who committed to wearing it until same-sex marriage was legal. People wore and shared the rings widely. The campaign ran during the Australian Marriage Law Postal Survey period.

## Why it worked
Brand as activist (P04): Airbnb's business is fundamentally about people feeling they belong anywhere — the stance was structurally consistent with the brand idea, not opportunistic. User as co-author (P09): the ring-wearers became the campaign medium. The physical object anchored a digital conversation.

## Steal
When a brand's core purpose is directly affected by a social or legal injustice, build a physical artefact that lets supporters signal the position without shouting. Objects persist longer than hashtags and require more commitment from participants — that commitment creates emotional investment.
