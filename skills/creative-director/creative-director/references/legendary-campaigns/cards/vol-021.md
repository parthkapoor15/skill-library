# Super. Human. (Channel 4 · 2021)

## Insight
Nine years after reframing Paralympians as elite athletes and expanding to all disabled people, the audience had absorbed that framing — the next truth to tell was the unglamorous one: the suffering, failures, and depression behind the performance.

## Mechanic
A film showing the unseen reality: training injuries, mental health crises, the monotony of rehabilitation, moments of genuine despair — alongside the eventual triumph. Athletes as human beings first, competitors second.

## Why it worked
The platform survived nine years because each iteration raised the level of honesty rather than the level of spectacle. Triumphal → normalizing → truthful is a coherent evolution that builds trust rather than fatigue.

## Steal
A long-running platform earns the right to harder truths over time. Use that accumulated trust to say what no earlier iteration could — the audience is ready for it because the brand built toward it.

# We're the Superhumans (Channel 4 · 2016)

## Insight
After "Meet the Superhumans" established Paralympic athletes as elite competitors, the next step was to expand the circle from heroic athletes to all disabled people in everyday life.

## Mechanic
A film featuring Paralympic athletes alongside disabled people in ordinary situations — playing music, working, cooking, laughing — all set to the song "Yes I Can." The scope deliberately expands beyond sport into daily existence.

## Why it worked
The sequel evolved the platform rather than repeating it. The first campaign said "these athletes are superhuman"; this campaign said "we are all superhuman." The expansion of the definition is the creative move — it prevents self-plagiarism and deepens the brand platform.

## Steal
When extending a successful campaign, the second execution must widen the circle of who the idea includes. Narrower sequels are self-copying; wider sequels are platform-building.

# Back to the Start (Chipotle · 2011)

## Insight
Modern factory farming began with farmers who had good intentions — they wanted to feed more people efficiently. Over time, efficiency became the only value. The story of industrial food is a story of mission drift. The insight: going "back to the start" is not nostalgia; it is ethics.

## Mechanic
A two-minute stop-motion animated film showing a farmer who builds a thriving traditional farm, then industrializes it — chickens become cogs, pigs become assembly-line units — until the factory is producing food he no longer recognizes. He tears it down and starts over, "back to the start." Scored with Willie Nelson's cover of "The Scientist" by Coldplay. First broadcast at the Grammy Awards, then released online where it accumulated tens of millions of views.

## Why it worked
P16 (long-form drama): the farmer's arc — aspiration, corruption, redemption — follows a complete narrative structure. P13 (truth telling): unlike most QSR advertising, this film tells a story about where food comes from and why it matters. Chipotle's supply chain makes this authentic rather than opportunistic. The Willie Nelson song anchors the film emotionally in American agrarian nostalgia without being sentimental.

## Steal
The redemption arc is the most powerful narrative structure in advertising because it requires the audience to believe that the protagonist recognizes their error. The farmer tearing down the factory farm is the most credible moment in the film — because the decision costs him something.

# The Scarecrow (Chipotle · 2013)

## Insight
Industrial food production is a horror story that most consumers prefer not to see — growth hormones, factory farms, processed food that looks nothing like its ingredients. Chipotle's entire business model is an argument against this system. The brand had the rare opportunity to make an ad that was actually truthful about what it was opposing.

## Mechanic
A four-minute animated film following a scarecrow through a dystopian corporate food system — Crow Foods Inc., where animals are factory-farmed and processed into food of disturbing abstraction. The scarecrow is complicit at first, then troubled, then defiant. He escapes to grow a small farm and sell real food. The film is wordless, set to a haunting cover of "Pure Imagination" from Charlie and the Chocolate Factory sung by Fiona Apple. The film launched simultaneously with a mobile game. 6.5 million YouTube views in three days.

## Why it worked
P16 (long-form drama): the emotional arc is complete — crisis of conscience, decision, action, resolution. The animation quality is feature-film level: every frame is designed to carry emotional weight. P13 (truth telling): Chipotle is telling the truth about a system it genuinely competes against. The brand has authentic standing to make this argument because its entire supply chain is built on rejecting what the film depicts. Won Cannes Grand Prix.

## Steal
When your brand's genuine competitive practice is the answer to a systemic problem, make the film about the problem. You do not need to appear in it. The audience will draw the connection when the problem is real and your solution is credible.

# The Last Da Vinci (Christie's · 2017)

## Insight
The Salvator Mundi — attributed to Leonardo da Vinci — was the rarest object on Earth: the last privately owned Leonardo, lost for centuries and rediscovered. The auction was not just a sale; it was a cultural event that the entire world had a stake in.

## Mechanic
A global media and events campaign built around the discovery, authentication, and auctioning of the Salvator Mundi. Cultural hijack (P05): the painting's story was so extraordinary that Christie's role was to frame and amplify it rather than create anything. User as co-author (P09): public exhibition tours, documentary films, and digital engagement made the auction a participatory cultural moment.

## Why it worked
The object's cultural power was so immense that the campaign's job was curation rather than creation. Cultural hijack (P05): Christie's attached itself to a story that was already world news. The $450 million final price was the ultimate proof of concept — though subsequent questions about authentication added complexity to the legacy.

## Steal
When you have access to a genuinely unique or historic object, product, or story, the campaign's job is to get out of the way and let the object do the work. Curation > creation. Give the world access to something extraordinary and they will do your marketing for you.
