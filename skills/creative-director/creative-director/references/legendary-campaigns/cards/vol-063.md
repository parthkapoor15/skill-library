# Laundrette (Levi's · 1985)

## Insight

In 1985, Levi's 501s were dying in the UK — associated with old men and workwear. The brand needed to be reborn as a symbol of young male desirability. Nick Kamen stripping in a laundrette, unselfconscious and unashamed, didn't sell jeans — it sold the idea of what wearing them could mean about you.

## Mechanic

A 60-second TV film: a young Nick Kamen walks into a 1950s American launderette, strips down to his boxer shorts, and puts his 501s in the wash. The women in the launderette watch with open desire. Marvin Gaye's "I Heard It Through the Grapevine" plays. No dialogue. The commercial aired once before generating 300,000 phone enquiries to Levi's. Boxer shorts sold out across the UK. Levi's sales rose 800% within a year.

## Why it worked

P08 (craft as message): Jonathan Glazer's precise period reconstruction transferred authenticity — if the brand could recreate this world this perfectly, it belonged to it. P16 (long-form drama) used restraint and silence as sexual tension. Nick Kamen's unselfconsciousness was the opposite of the hard-sell swagger that characterized men's advertising — vulnerability as desirability. The commercial essentially invented a new template for male aspirational advertising in the UK.

## Steal

The most powerful product claim can often be made without words, without the product being the visual focus, and without anyone explaining what the claim is. Find the situation that makes the audience feel the benefit rather than understand it.

# Odyssey (Levi's · 2016)

## Insight
Levi's is worn by people who make things, go places, and don't ask permission. A cinematic film about movement, grit, and the American spirit — without ever showing the product prominently — communicates more than product specs ever could.

## Mechanic
A visually ambitious long-form film following characters on a journey, shot with cinematic craft. Brand presence minimal (P07); emotional arc carries the brand claim.

## Why it worked
P07 and P16 work together when the brand is already culturally legible. Levi's can afford to disappear because its cultural meaning (freedom, authenticity, work) is pre-loaded. The film earned organic attention through visual quality rather than media spend.

## Steal
When the brand is already a cultural symbol, long-form cinematic work with minimal branding can operate as cultural content rather than advertising. The less you explain, the more you imply.

# The Refugee Edition (Libération · 2015)

## Insight
News coverage of the refugee crisis showed statistics and tragedy from a distance. What would it mean for refugees to have their own voice in a mainstream publication — not as subjects of coverage but as authors?

## Mechanic
French newspaper Libération handed over an entire edition to refugees — they wrote articles, took photographs, and told their own stories. The newspaper's design language remained intact; only the bylines changed. The edition was distributed normally.

## Why it worked
The inversion of subject/author is the truth-telling mechanism. Design as idea (P17) makes the argument visible: the same newspaper, the same design, but different voices — demonstrating that the gap between refugee and journalist is human circumstance, not inherent difference.

## Steal
Find the one editorial or production decision that, if changed, would make your brand's stated values structurally visible rather than rhetorically stated. Then make that decision publicly.

# DILL (LIDL · 2022)

## Insight
Dill is Sweden's most culturally loaded herb — beloved, omnipresent, slightly absurd in its ubiquity. LIDL, as a discount retailer trying to earn Swedish cultural legitimacy, could stake a claim to this humble, ridiculous piece of Swedish identity.

## Mechanic
A film treating LIDL's dill — packaged, sold, Swedish — with the gravity and production quality of a luxury brand launch. Documentary-style reverence applied to a 15-kronor bunch of herbs. Design-driven: the typography and visual identity of the film were themselves the joke.

## Why it worked
The contrast between prestige execution (luxury brand launch aesthetics) and humble subject (discount store dill) was the entire concept. By treating Sweden's cheapest herb as Sweden's most important cultural export, LIDL positioned itself as a brand with genuine Swedish cultural fluency rather than just competitive prices.

## Steal
When a budget brand needs cultural credibility, find the unpretentious, humble, locally specific thing the category ignores and elevate it with maximum craft. The gap between production quality and subject matter is the joke, the insight, and the brand positioning simultaneously.

# Big Dreams (Little Caesars · 2018)

## Insight
Little Caesars' Hot-N-Ready value proposition is unambiguous: cheap, fast, ready. Rather than apologize for the positioning, lean into absurdist humor that celebrates the simple joy of accessible pizza.

## Mechanic
A comedic film in which characters articulate hilariously mundane or absurdly deflated "big dreams" — the joke being the gap between the grandiosity implied by the phrase and the small, affordable satisfaction the product delivers.

## Why it worked
Absurd as carrier (P11): the joke lives in the gap between inflated expectation (big dreams) and humble, satisfying reality (Hot-N-Ready pizza). The brand is honest about what it is and makes that honesty funny rather than defensive.

## Steal
Own your price-value positioning with humor rather than defensiveness. The absurdist framing makes affordability charming rather than cheap. Honest positioning + comedy = brand confidence.
