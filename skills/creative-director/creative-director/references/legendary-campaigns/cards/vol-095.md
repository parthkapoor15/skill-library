# Inheritance (Skoda · 2008)

## Insight
Cars are passed down through families like heirlooms — sometimes with affection, sometimes with mixed feelings. The emotional relationship to an inherited car is rarely simple, and rarely acknowledged in automotive advertising.

## Mechanic
A comedic film exploring the mixed emotions of inheriting a family car — the obligation, the sentiment, the slight embarrassment — in Skoda's warm-but-wry tonal register.

## Why it worked
Automotive advertising typically shows aspirational new car ownership. The inheritance scenario inverted the aspiration: this is a car you have because of someone else's affection, not your own choice. The human specificity made it memorable in a category of generic aspiration.

## Steal
The most underused angle in category advertising is the awkward, ambivalent human truth about the product's role in real life. Aspiration is crowded; ambivalence is empty.

# Love Story (Skoda · 2010)

## Insight
The relationship between a driver and their car is genuinely emotional — but automotive advertising usually either ignores this (feature focus) or over-romanticises it (epic lifestyle shots). Skoda's register: affectionate, slightly self-aware, never portentous.

## Mechanic
A film treating the car-owner relationship as a genuine love story — with all the awkward, specific, domestic intimacy of real relationships rather than the cinematic sweep of automotive advertising convention.

## Why it worked
The warmth of the love story register, applied to cars without ironic distance, captured something authentic about why people care about their vehicles — without the pretension that typically accompanies this territory.

## Steal
Automotive brands that aren't premium can own the territory of genuine human affection rather than aspiration. Real love — imperfect, specific, domestic — is more memorable than aspirational lifestyle advertising.

# The Best Planet (Skyscanner · 2020)

## Insight
Travel brands always sell "the best destination." Skyscanner's insight: Earth is the only planet you can actually fly to, so technically every flight is going to "the best planet available." The reduction of the aspirational travel cliché to absurd literalism is the joke.

## Mechanic
A film or campaign treating Earth as a travel destination in competition with other planets — using the language of travel marketing applied to Earth itself, as if the audience needed convincing this is the right planet to visit.

## Why it worked
P11 (absurd as carrier) via a premise that inverts travel advertising's grandiosity: instead of "this is the world's best destination," the brand asks a more fundamental question and answers it with comic literalness. P17 elements in the visual language of planetary comparison. The meta-awareness of travel category clichés is charming rather than cynical.

## Steal
Take your category's most grandiose standard claim and reduce it to its most literal absurd form. The gap between the inflated claim and the literal reality is where the comedy and the honesty meet.

# Visit Earth (Skyscanner · 2020)

## Insight
Companion to "The Best Planet" — same absurdist engine, second execution. Earth as travel destination being sold to prospective visitors in the language of tourist board marketing.

## Mechanic
A tourism-board-style film promoting Earth as a destination, complete with the aspirational language and visual clichés of travel advertising, delivered with the self-awareness that makes the joke land rather than feel cynical.

## Why it worked
P11 (absurd as carrier) applied consistently: same engine as "The Best Planet" — tourist board language applied to Earth itself. The series format works because each execution is a new manifestation of the same absurdist premise without repeating the exact same joke.

## Steal
Series absurdism works when the engine generates genuinely different jokes, not just the same joke in different settings. "Best Planet" and "Visit Earth" are two different angles on the same premise — alien perspective vs. tourist board register.

# Txtbks (Smart Communications · 2012)

## Insight
In the Philippines, public schools faced a critical shortage of textbooks — many schools had a ratio of one textbook per several students. Meanwhile, SMS text message technology was ubiquitous and essentially free for Smart's subscribers. Children could receive text messages on basic phones but couldn't access textbooks.

## Mechanic
Smart partnered with the Department of Education to encode entire school textbook content into SMS format — the character constraints of text messaging forced the content into compressed, scannable units. Textbooks were compressed and sent as free SMS messages to students' phones. The same character limit that makes texting constraining became the format for educational content. Cannes Grand Prix winner.

## Why it worked
P17 (design as idea): the SMS format constraints became the design solution — shortening and simplifying textbook content made it more accessible, not less educational. P03 (behavior inversion): the conventional wisdom that textbooks require paper and printing was inverted; the phone's limitation (character count) became an advantage. The campaign generated value not through advertising but through genuinely solving an educational infrastructure problem with existing telecom infrastructure.

## Steal
Find where a technology constraint that is typically a problem (character limits, low bandwidth, small screens) maps exactly onto a real-world constraint (insufficient textbooks, limited distribution). The constraint is the solution. Tech-as-infrastructure-not-advertising is more durable than tech-as-advertising.
