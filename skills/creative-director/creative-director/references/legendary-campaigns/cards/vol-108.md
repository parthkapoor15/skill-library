# The Ride (unknown · 2018)

## Insight
TODO: Verify brand. A long-form film using the metaphor of a journey — physical or otherwise — to explore a human truth about connection, transition, or the passage of time.

## Mechanic
A long-form narrative film built around a shared journey — a car trip, a commute, a road — that becomes the setting for a human story about connection, loss, or change. The product's role emerges through the context of the journey.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16): the journey format has built-in narrative architecture — departure, progress, arrival — that provides structure without requiring complex plot. Truth telling (P13): if the human story rings true, the product gains credibility by association. Journey films are universal because everyone understands the emotional grammar of leaving and arriving.

## Steal
The journey is the most reliable narrative container for technology and telecom because it is inherently about connection across distance. Who someone calls, what they use along the way, who they come home to — these moments write themselves.

# This is How We Work Now (Upwork · 2021)

## Insight

The pandemic permanently changed work — remote and freelance is not a compromise, it's the new normal. Old corporate structures feel like corpses insisting they're still alive.

## Mechanic

Musical spot featuring a literal animated corpse singing about the "old way of working" being dead — absurdist imagery celebrating the shift to freelance and distributed work as progress, not disruption.

## Why it worked

Absurdist drama with precise strategic clarity: the corpse metaphor made an abstract category shift (remote work becoming mainstream) viscerally concrete. Upwork positioned itself as the brand of the new world, not a tool for a transition.

## Steal

When a category is undergoing irreversible change, be the first brand to declare the old order dead — with a visual that makes the death undeniable rather than merely stated.

# If You're Into It, It's in the V&A (Victoria & Albert Museum · 2024)

## Insight
The V&A holds one of the world's most diverse collections — fashion, gaming, jewellery, theatre, technology — but is perceived as a highbrow institution for a narrow cultural elite.

## Mechanic
A poster campaign using the typographic format of internet subculture — bold, democratic, direct — connecting specific niche interests ("If you're into sneakers…", "If you're into K-pop…") directly to V&A collections. Design language borrowed from street culture, not heritage institutions.

## Why it worked
The message (everything is here) was delivered through the visual language of the people who thought it wasn't for them. The design inversion was the argument: the institution that looks like this cannot be stuffy.

## Steal
Adopt the visual vernacular of your most alienated potential audience to communicate the message that the institution contains their world. Design style is the proof; copy is the invitation.

# 48 Hours (Verizon Droid · 2010)

## Insight
TODO: Verify year. Part of Verizon's Droid campaign era — positioning Android against iPhone. A battery life or capability demonstration using 48-hour continuous use as the proof point.

## Mechanic
An absurdist spot using 48 hours of continuous use as the premise — likely showing increasingly extreme scenarios over a 48-hour period where the Droid remains functional, contrasted against implicitly failing competitors.

## Why it worked
Battery life and durability as product claims are unconvincing when stated but compelling when demonstrated through extreme use scenarios. The 48-hour framing gave specificity that "all-day battery" lacked.

## Steal
Vague durability claims ("long-lasting," "reliable") become memorable when given a specific time frame and an absurd use case. The specificity of "48 hours" is more believable than an unquantified claim.
