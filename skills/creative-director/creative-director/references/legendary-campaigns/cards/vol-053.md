# Meet Marina Prieto (JCDecaux · 2024)

## Insight
Loneliness among the elderly is invisible — it has no image, no protest, no campaign. Marina Prieto, 85, placed a handwritten ad in a local shop window looking for friends. It was the most honest advertising anyone had seen.

## Mechanic
JCDecaux, an outdoor advertising company, amplified Marina's handwritten ad onto their billboards across Spain. The same handwritten font, the same personal tone, the same phone number — but now visible to millions. The response was overwhelming: calls, letters, visits, new friendships.

## Why it worked
The truth-telling (P13) is Marina's own voice, unchanged and amplified. JCDecaux's role is pure infrastructure — they provided the scale without altering the message. The participation mechanic (P09): the audience's response (calling Marina) IS the campaign's success metric. The brand demonstrates its power by disappearing behind someone else's story.

## Steal
Find the most honest ad that already exists in the world — handwritten, undesigned, urgent. Amplify it without changing a word. Your production budget is invisible; their authenticity is irreplaceable.

# The Great Schlep (JCER · 2008)

## Insight
Jewish grandparents in Florida were a decisive voting bloc for Obama — but they were being influenced more by what their grandchildren said than by any political advertising. The shortest path to winning Florida was through family dinner tables, not TV.

## Mechanic
Sarah Silverman starred in a video instructing young Jewish Americans to visit their grandparents in Florida and convince them to vote for Obama. Absurdist, irreverent, and culturally specific — it used guilt, love, and comedy to assign a political mission. The call to action was a plane ticket and a weekend.

## Why it worked
It found the real influence map, not the assumed one. Political communication usually goes direct to voters; this went to the people voters actually listen to. Silverman's tone — funny but sincere — gave cover for an earnest message that would have been insufferable if played straight.

## Steal
Before building a broadcast campaign, map the actual social influence network. Who do your targets actually change their behaviour for? Often it's not your media plan.

# Snow Covered (Jeep · 2020)

## Insight

Every Jeep left parked in snow carries a ghost image of its own silhouette — the most minimal proof of the vehicle's shape. Snow doesn't obscure a Jeep; it frames it.

## Mechanic

OOH/print campaign using photographs of snow-covered Jeeps where the accumulated snow perfectly traces the vehicle outline, functioning as a natural stencil of the Jeep silhouette. No copy beyond the Jeep logo. The snow itself becomes the art direction.

## Why it worked

P17 (design as idea): the visual is the concept — no explanation needed. P08 (craft as message): the photographs are both documentary and artful, transferring a sense of environmental authenticity to the brand. Works harder in winter markets where the audience literally experiences the same phenomenon in their own driveways.

## Steal

Find the natural phenomenon that occurs around your product in its real environment. Photograph it without intervention. If the product is doing something beautiful when no one is watching, that is your best ad.

# Parallels (Jim Beam · 2011)

## Insight
Bourbon is not just a drink — it's a generational inheritance. The rituals men repeat with whiskey are often unconsciously identical to those of their fathers and grandfathers: the pour, the glass, the company. That inheritance is worth making visible.

## Mechanic
Long-form film interweaving parallel moments across generations — fathers and sons sharing rituals of work, craft, and drink — demonstrating continuity without sentimentality. The craft of the filmmaking itself mirrors the craftsmanship story.

## Why it worked
The parallel editing structure was the idea, not just the execution. By literally showing the same moment across different decades, the film made the abstract (generational connection) physically visible without stating it.

## Steal
When your brand story is about continuity over time, parallel editing is not a stylistic choice — it's an argument. Structure mirrors content.

# Buster the Boxer (John Lewis · 2016)

## Insight
The excitement of a new Christmas present is most purely expressed by the least inhibited recipient — an animal seeing a trampoline for the first time.

## Mechanic
A film where a father secretly builds a trampoline in the garden on Christmas Eve. Various garden animals — foxes, badgers, squirrels — discover and play on it during the night. On Christmas morning, his daughter runs to the trampoline — and is beaten to it by the family dog, Buster.

## Why it worked
The comedy comes from the dog's uncontained glee — the honest version of what Christmas morning excitement looks like. The reveal subverts expectations (animals are the stars; the dog steals the ending). Accessible, shareable joy without sentimentality.

## Steal
Cast the most emotionally uninhibited version of your insight as the protagonist. Animals and children express desire and joy without self-consciousness — their reactions are more credible than performed adult emotions.
