# Boost Your Voice (Boost Mobile · 2020)

## Insight
Black communities are systematically underrepresented in elections partly due to polling station closures. Boost Mobile's stores were already located in underserved Black communities. The stores could do something about it.

## Mechanic
Boost Mobile converted its retail stores in underserved communities into polling stations for the 2020 US election. The brand literally used its physical footprint to expand voting access — becoming infrastructure for civic participation.

## Why it worked
Brand as activist (P04): the action was structural, not symbolic — stores became polling stations, not billboards about voting. User as co-author (P09): the community participated by voting; the brand provided the mechanism. The connection between the brand's actual asset (stores in underserved areas) and the social problem (voting access) was airtight.

## Steal
Identify what physical or operational asset your brand owns that could solve a real civic or community problem. The most credible activism converts existing brand infrastructure into social good — not a check or a campaign, but actual function.

# A British Original (British Airways · 2022)

## Insight
When an airline loses its premium status, the temptation is to sell destinations — but British Airways found that the real differentiator was the people flying: odd, stubborn, specific British individuals who couldn't exist anywhere else.

## Mechanic
A campaign of portrait films and OOH featuring real passengers and staff — their quirks, contradictions, and unapologetic Britishness — shot with restraint by Uncommon. Logo frequently cropped or minimal. Ran across cinema, OOH, and digital.

## Why it worked
In a category where every airline sells aspirational destinations equally, BA sold identity. The invisible-brand restraint (P07) signals confidence — only a truly established brand can afford to put a partial logo on 324 billboard sites. The specificity of British character gave international audiences a point of fascination rather than a generic premium promise.

## Steal
When your category is visually homogeneous, abandon category codes entirely and anchor in national or local characterology. Crop your own logo — only secure brands can do it, and the act itself communicates hierarchy.

# Magic of Flying (British Airways · 2013)

## Insight
We have lost the wonder of flight. Humans flying in metal tubes at 35,000 feet is, objectively, extraordinary — but familiarity has made it routine. British Airways understood that the moment wonder could be restored, the brand would be differentiated in a category where everyone offers the same routes and similar prices.

## Mechanic
An interactive digital billboard on Piccadilly Circus and Chiswick that, using live flight data, tracks real British Airways flights passing overhead. When a BA plane flies over the billboard, a child digital character runs out and points up at the actual plane in the actual sky above. The plane's flight number and origin city appear. Passersby stop and look up at the real plane. A real aircraft becomes part of a live advertisement.

## Why it worked
P18 (tech as canvas): live flight tracking data is the technology; the experience it enables — pointing a child at a real plane overhead — is impossible without it. The technology does not decorate the idea; the technology is the idea. P09 (user as co-author): passersby become participants when they look up. The billboard directs their attention to the sky, not to the screen. Won three Cannes Grand Prix. Set a new standard for ambient outdoor technology in advertising.

## Steal
Find the real-world data stream that, if made visible at the right moment and place, creates wonder. The aircraft data existed before the campaign — British Airways did not create it. The creative act was connecting it to a moment of public attention in real time.

# Windows (British Airways · 2024)

## Insight
The most emotionally charged moment of any flight is not the meal, the seat, or the destination — it's the pause at the window: a private, unspoken experience that no airline had ever thought to sell.

## Mechanic
Eleven large-format OOH prints showing close-cropped faces of passengers at aircraft windows, expressions of wonder or reflection. British Airways logo partially cut off by the frame — a deliberate act of brand confidence. 324 placements across the UK.

## Why it worked
Limitation as power (P06): the crop communicates that BA is so recognizable it doesn't need to show its logo in full. Design as idea (P17): the image does all the work — no headline required. The invisible-brand confidence (P07) operates through pure restraint.

## Steal
If your brand is genuinely famous, crop your logo deliberately. The act of truncation communicates status more efficiently than any superlative headline. Weaker competitors cannot imitate it without looking incompetent.

# Effortless (Brylcreem · 2010)

## Insight
Men's grooming products operate in a paradox: the goal is to look like you've made no effort, while actually having made significant effort. "Effortless" is both the product's aspiration and a knowing acknowledgment of the effort required to appear effortless.

## Mechanic
A film combining absurdist humor (P11) with long-form storytelling (P16) to explore the "effortless" paradox — likely showing the extreme effort required to achieve apparent effortlessness, as a comedic demonstration of the product's efficacy.

## Why it worked
The effortlessness paradox is a genuine male grooming truth that the category rarely acknowledges. A brand that names this truth with humor earns credibility. The longer format allows the joke to develop its own logic rather than being delivered as a punchline.

## Steal
In categories with internal paradoxes (effort to appear effortless, natural ingredients in processed food), naming the paradox with humor is more credible than pretending it doesn't exist.
