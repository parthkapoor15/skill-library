# Project Tokyo (Meta · 2021)

## Insight
The Tokyo Olympics were held without spectators due to COVID-19. Athletes competed in silence. Families who trained alongside their athletes for years could not witness their defining moments. Facebook/Meta could be the connection point across that distance.

## Mechanic
A film or campaign built around athletes at the Tokyo Olympics and the loved ones who could not attend — showing how the platform sustained the emotional connection across quarantine and travel restrictions. The pandemic context made the theme of separation and reunion viscerally resonant.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16): the emotional stakes of the Olympics without spectators required extended treatment. The timing was inherently newsworthy — the empty Olympic stadium was the image of 2021. Connection across distance is Facebook's core claim, and the context made that claim necessary rather than aspirational.

## Steal
Look for moments in the real world where your product's core benefit is desperately needed. Don't create the context — find it. The COVID Olympics was a gift to any brand whose product is human connection.

# Broadway Poem (MetLife · 2002)

## Insight
After 9/11, institutional advertising felt obscene. The only credible tone for a brand with New York roots was silence, or something close to it — testimony rather than message.

## Mechanic
A film using the streets of Broadway as its canvas, structured as a visual poem — typography and images from the city itself carrying the emotional weight without conventional advertising structure or explicit brand message.

## Why it worked
In a moment of collective grief, the brands that spoke least spoke loudest. The typographic, poetic register signalled that this was not an ad trying to capitalize — it was a brand bearing witness.

## Steal
In moments of genuine cultural crisis, the question is not "what do we say?" but "what register is appropriate?" Sometimes the register is poetry, not persuasion.

# Post-9/11 Broadway Tribute (MetLife · 2001)

## Insight
New York's Broadway district was the heart of the city's identity before September 11. A tribute anchored there carried weight that no generic memorial could — it was specific, local, and earned.

## Mechanic
A film tribute using Broadway's visual and cultural heritage — the marquees, the street, the performers — as the location for commemoration. Minimal copy, maximum specificity to place. The geography did the emotional work.

## Why it worked
Grief needs specificity to feel real. A generic memorial film about "America" would have been invisible. Anchoring the tribute to a specific street, a specific district, a specific cultural identity gave it emotional density.

## Steal
In memorial communication, specificity is respect. Generic remembrance is forgettable. Name the street. Name the people. The narrower the geography, the wider the feeling.

# Dumb Ways to Die (Metro Trains Melbourne · 2012)

## Insight
Rail safety campaigns use fear to change behaviour — and fail, because people habituate to fear-based messaging and disengage. The insight: the same information delivered through joy and absurdism reaches an audience that has learned to tune out warnings.

## Mechanic
An animated music video of cute cartoon characters dying in increasingly absurd ways, culminating in three deaths caused by train-safety stupidity. The song became a global hit, the video was shared hundreds of millions of times, and the characters became a franchise — apps, merchandise, sequels. A rail safety campaign that people chose to consume.

## Why it worked
P11 (absurd as carrier) at maximum strength: death — the most serious subject — delivered as cheerful animation with a catchy tune. The cognitive dissonance between cute visual execution and the actual subject (gruesome death) creates the laugh, the share, and the memory. P10 (serialization): the characters became a recurring franchise that kept the safety message alive long after the initial launch. The campaign reduced platform incidents by 30% in the first year.

## Steal
When your audience has become immune to the conventional register of your message (fear, urgency, warning), switch to the least appropriate register. The more serious the underlying message, the greater the attention earned by treating it as comedy.

# Dreamcaster (Michelob Ultra · 2024)

## Insight

Blind sports fans cannot experience basketball the way sighted fans do — play-by-play commentary doesn't capture the spatial relationships, body language, and visual drama that make the game great. They deserve better.

## Mechanic

Michelob Ultra trained and deployed AI that provided spatial audio commentary for visually impaired NBA fans — translating the court's visual geometry into 3D audio that let blind fans experience the spatial reality of play for the first time.

## Why it worked

Tech as canvas meets truth telling: a genuine accessibility gap was addressed with technology that delivered a genuinely better experience — not an accommodation, but an improvement. Michelob Ultra's "championing of sport enjoyment" brand platform was demonstrated through action, not claimed through words.

## Steal

Find the audience your category has structurally excluded and use available technology to serve them with something genuinely better than what everyone else gets. The story of inclusion earns media; the product earns loyalty.
