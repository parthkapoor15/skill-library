# Like a Girl (Always · 2014)

## Insight
"Like a girl" is used as an insult — implying weakness, inefficiency, inadequacy — at the exact developmental moment when girls are becoming aware of their own limitations. The phrase creates the limitation it describes. Older girls have already absorbed it; young girls haven't.

## Mechanic
A social experiment asking subjects of different ages to demonstrate running, throwing, and fighting "like a girl." Older subjects (teens, adults) perform weakly — they've internalised the insult. Young girls perform at full capacity. The gap between the two groups was the campaign. Ran as a film during the Super Bowl and online simultaneously.

## Why it worked
The social experiment format produced undeniable, unrehearsed evidence of the insight rather than a stated argument. The contrast between the two groups didn't need commentary — it arrived as direct observation. Always had genuine standing (a feminine care brand for girls entering puberty) and the insight connected product directly to purpose.

## Steal
Social experiment campaigns are only credible if the result could have gone the other way. The "Like a Girl" experiment couldn't be scripted because it depended on real people's internalised assumptions. Design experiments that genuinely reveal rather than confirm.

# Great Shows Stay With You (Amazon Prime · 2022)

## Insight
In a world of content abundance, the problem is not access — it is emotional connection. The shows that matter are the ones that leave a trace: a scene you replay, a character you grieve, a line you quote years later. That trace is the product.

## Mechanic
Campaign films depicting how great television moments physically stay with viewers — characters from beloved shows appearing in their everyday lives, haunting them in the best possible way. Long-form drama (P16) executes the emotional logic of the insight.

## Why it worked
Long-form drama (P16): the extended runtime allows genuine emotional investment rather than a snapshot. The insight is true — great shows do stay with you — and the mechanic literalises that truth visually. User as co-author (P09): viewers recognize their own relationship with beloved shows in the portrayal.

## Steal
When selling a content platform, sell the emotional residue of the content, not the content itself. The memory is more valuable to the audience than the catalogue. Make them remember something they felt rather than describe something they could watch.

# Small Business Saturday (American Express · 2010)

## Insight
Black Friday and Cyber Monday had colonized the post-Thanksgiving shopping calendar, funneling billions to large retailers and e-commerce platforms. Small businesses — the backbone of local communities — had no equivalent cultural moment. American Express cards were accepted more often at small businesses than at chain retailers; AmEx's business interest aligned with small business survival.

## Mechanic
AmEx invented a new national shopping holiday: the Saturday between Black Friday and Cyber Monday. The idea: shop local that day. AmEx provided free Facebook advertising to small businesses, created shop-local maps, and drove the hashtag. The campaign grew from a single-year stunt into an officially recognized national day — US Senate passed a resolution supporting it. Over $5.5B was spent at small businesses on the day in its peak years.

## Why it worked
AmEx didn't create an ad — it created a calendar slot. P04 (brand as activist): the sacrifice was real: AmEx funded competitor-free advertising for small merchants and positioned itself against the large retailers where AmEx cards are also accepted. P10 (serialization): the genius was creating something annual and repeatable, with growing institutional recognition. The Senate resolution was the credibility that advertising money could never buy.

## Steal
When you cannot out-spend competitors for existing cultural moments, invent one. Find the gap in the cultural calendar that serves a constituency your brand authentically represents. Design it to be legible in three words, repeatable annually, and genuinely useful to the community — not just promotionally useful to the brand.

# World's Toughest Job (American Greetings · 2014)

## Insight
Mothers are described in sentimental terms in every Mother's Day campaign — warm, loving, self-sacrificing. The reality of motherhood, listed as a job description with its actual demands, would be recognized as impossible. The insight: no one would take this job — and yet every mother does, and does it without being paid or thanked.

## Mechanic
A fake job interview for a "director of operations" position with impossible requirements: no breaks, no vacations, 135+ hours per week, no pay, degree in medicine/finance/culinary arts required. Candidates are shown the requirements on screen during the "interview." They react with appropriate horror. The reveal: the job is being a mom. The candidates are visibly moved. American Greetings positioned its Mother's Day cards as the least mothers deserve.

## Why it worked
P13 (truth-telling) through the specific language of employment — a register that made the familiar unfamiliar. By translating motherhood into HR terms, the film made visible what emotional language had made invisible through overuse. P11 (absurd) operated in the setup: the idea of a job interview for motherhood is inherently absurd. The reveal worked because it was a genuine reframe, not just a sentiment. The film was shared extensively because it gave people a new way to articulate something they felt but hadn't said precisely.

## Steal
Take a sentiment that has been expressed in emotional language so many times it has become invisible, and re-express it in a completely different register (bureaucratic, legal, scientific). The new register makes the familiar feel urgent again.

# Trial by Timeline (Amnesty International · 2012)

## Insight
In repressive regimes, social media posts are used as evidence of political opposition — making a Facebook timeline the equivalent of a criminal file. Turning that threat into a visible demonstration on the very platform that creates the risk was pointed and immediate.

## Mechanic
A platform that converted users' Facebook timelines into a mock arrest warrant, using their own public posts as "evidence" — the same way authoritarian governments actually do. Users could share their "warrant" to spread awareness of how social media is used as surveillance evidence in repressive states.

## Why it worked
The mechanic was the message. By putting the user in the position of the accused — using their own words as the evidence — Amnesty made an abstract international rights issue personal and immediate. The participation was not performative; it was genuinely unsettling.

## Steal
When the injustice you're campaigning against uses a familiar mechanism (social media, data, technology) against people, the most powerful demonstration is turning that same mechanism on your audience — briefly, safely, with permission — so they understand what it feels like.
