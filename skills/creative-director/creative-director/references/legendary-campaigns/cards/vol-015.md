# Burning Stores (Burger King · 2017)

## Insight
Burger King's Flame Grilling is the brand's defining product truth — and the most compelling proof of that truth is a restaurant actually on fire. Burger King has had multiple real locations catch fire over the years.

## Mechanic
Burger King ran print ads using real photographs of Burger King restaurants that had caught fire — framed as aspirational product photography. The burning building demonstrates the flame-grilling claim better than any stylised food shot. "Flame grilled since 1954."

## Why it worked
Behaviour inversion (P03): using disaster photography as brand advertising inverts every convention of QSR communication. Design as idea (P17): the image choice IS the argument — no copy required beyond the tagline. The audacity signals total brand confidence. The fires were real; the framing is the idea.

## Steal
Search your brand's archive for the moments that were disasters at the time. Then ask: does this disaster demonstrate the brand truth more honestly than any planned shoot? If yes, license it.

# Confusing Times (Burger King · 2020)

## Insight
2020 was universally described as unprecedented, confusing, and chaotic. Rather than pivot to seriousness, Burger King chose to acknowledge the chaos with characteristic absurdism — the brand's cultural position is irreverence, not earnestness.

## Mechanic
A series of Burger King communications during 2020 used the language of "confusing times" to frame their promotions, menu items, and brand communications through the lens of collective cultural bewilderment. The campaign acknowledged the moment without exploiting it.

## Why it worked
Cultural hijack (P05) that doesn't overreach: Burger King claimed the cultural moment of shared confusion without attaching it to tragedy. The absurdist brand voice (P11) makes acknowledgment of chaos feel like the brand's natural register rather than crisis communication.

## Steal
In cultural moments where every brand is pivoting to seriousness, consider whether your brand's authentic voice is earnest or ironic. If it's ironic, staying ironic during a serious moment is more honest than performing seriousness.

# Google Home of the Whopper (Burger King · 2017)

## Insight
Burger King had 15 seconds in a TV ad — not enough time to describe all the Whopper's ingredients. Google Home devices, sitting in millions of living rooms, could read out the entire Wikipedia entry for the Whopper if triggered by a voice command. The audience was already equipped with the solution.

## Mechanic
A Burger King TV ad ended with an actor saying "OK Google, what is the Whopper burger?" — triggering Google Home devices in viewers' homes to read the Whopper's Wikipedia page aloud. Burger King then edited the Whopper's Wikipedia page to be more promotional. Chaos and coverage followed.

## Why it worked
Format as idea (P01): the 15-second TV ad is structurally incomplete without the Google Home device. They are one ad together. The audacity of activating a competitor's hardware as your own advertising medium generated global coverage. The Wikipedia manipulation added a second act.

## Steal
Audit every connected device in your audience's home or pocket. Find the one that, if triggered by your ad, would extend your message into their space without media spend. Then trigger it.

# The McWhopper Proposal (Burger King · 2015)

## Insight
Burger King and McDonald's are the most famous rivals in the world — but World Peace Day creates a cultural moment where a truce would be more newsworthy than any conventional ad.

## Mechanic
Burger King published an open letter to McDonald's CEO proposing a one-day truce: open a joint pop-up on World Peace Day, sell a hybrid burger (the McWhopper) and donate proceeds to Peace One Day. McDonald's declined publicly. BK won either way.

## Why it worked
McDonald's had no good response: accepting meant sharing the PR spotlight; declining made them the villain on World Peace Day. Burger King designed a public invitation where the competitor's only options both benefitted the challenger.

## Steal
Design a public invitation where the larger competitor has no favourable response. The chess move is the campaign — the competitor's reaction is the media.

# Moldy Whopper (Burger King · 2020)

## Insight
Food that never rots is not food — it is a chemical product engineered to simulate food. Every day a burger doesn't mould is evidence of the preservatives inside it. Burger King's decision to remove artificial preservatives produces a Whopper that rots naturally. That's the proof.

## Mechanic
Burger King filmed a Whopper decomposing over 34 days — beautiful time-lapse photography of progressive mould colonisation. The campaign ran as film, OOH, and print. No appetite appeal. No product shot at its best. Only the evidence of natural ingredients doing what natural ingredients do.

## Why it worked
Behaviour inversion (P03): showing a product at its worst to prove a quality claim inverts every QSR convention. Design as idea (P17): the rotting Whopper photograph is impossible to look away from — the repulsion IS the memorability. The message requires no copy: a rotting burger proves no artificial preservatives better than any ingredient list.

## Steal
Find the most counterintuitive way to show your product's most important quality claim. If the claim is "natural," the proof might be showing nature doing what nature does — including the parts that make audiences uncomfortable.
